# What's new

What changed in each version, newest first. Every update keeps your worlds as they are, and makes a backup of a library before changing how it is stored.

## 0.9.28

- **Ships.** A fifth kind of plan: a hull and its decks, with masts, a helm, cabins, a hold and ladders between decks, as a boat, a ship or a great ship. Key it, change it by hand, and take it away as a picture like any other plan.
- **The lie of the land.** Any plan can be on ground that is not ordinary: **overgrown** (growth through everything), **shattered** (a rift across it), **aloft** (an island in the sky), **adrift** (fragments in the mist, with lettered doors that open on rooms not next door), **unearthly** (nothing straight) or **the void** (a black sheet of stars). A ship is at sea, in the sky or among the stars.
- The app offers the ground a record's words suggest: "the Floating City" is drawn aloft, a place in a realm called "The Ethereal Realm" adrift.
- Plans you have already kept are not changed.

## 0.9.27

- **Plans join up.** Under a plan's key, **Joined plans** shows the place it is in, what leads to it and what it leads to, with a link each way.
- **A level below.** **Add a level below** makes the next level down as a plan of its own: tunnels under an inn's cellar, caves under a keep, the third level of a dungeon. Stairs are drawn where the way down is, and each plan says how it is reached from the other.
- **A place inside a place.** Tie a numbered place to a record of its own, or press **Make it one**, and its plan is a click away: the inn in the town, with the town's plan saying which number it is.
- **Way on**, a new kind of entry in the key: where a place leads and how, with a link to that place's plan.
- **Towns and cities in districts.** Each district is named for what stands in it, and you can call it what you like. **A plan of its own** draws a district closer, with the same streets and houses, to key and to change by hand.

## 0.9.26

- **Change a plan by hand.** **Change it by hand**, under the drawing, gives you tools: draw a room, move or resize one, cut a passage a square at a time, rub floor out, put a door (or a secret door, an archway, a portcullis, a window) in a wall, and set down stairs, pillars, altars, chests, beds and the rest. A new room gets a number and a place in the key.
- **Ready-made pieces.** Set down a guard post, a shrine, a row of cells, a crypt, a vault, a great hall and more, whole, turned as you like, and join them up.
- **Keep the rooms you like and redraw the rest.** In a dungeon, mark rooms **Keep**, then **Redraw the rest**: they stay as they are while everything else is laid out again around them. **Furnish it again** sets one room's contents down afresh, to suit the name it has now.
- **Settlements too.** Move, turn, resize, add and remove buildings, and give any house a number and a place in the key.
- **Undo and redo**, and everything works from the keyboard: the arrow keys move over the plan a square at a time, and Enter uses the tool.
- A plan changed by hand is its own drawing from then on, kept as you change it. **Start again** goes back to a plan made afresh.

## 0.9.25

- **A plan's places can be keyed.** Click a number on the plan and say what is there: words to read aloud, and any number of encounters, traps, puzzles, hazards, loot, secrets, clues, people and notes. Add, change and remove them as you like.
- **Suggest** offers an encounter from your books for the party and the place, loot to match its wealth, or a trap, a puzzle or a hazard with a DC to fit.
- An encounter's creatures open their stat blocks in the key, and **Put in the tracker** starts the fight.
- **Rename, add, remove and reorder** a plan's rooms in the key itself. Renaming a room no longer redraws the plan.
- Your copy of the plan marks what is keyed to each room. The key is kept as you write it, and **Write the key into the record** puts all of it into the record's text, for you only.

## 0.9.24

- **Plans of places.** Open a town, a building or a dungeon and choose **Draw a plan**: the app lays it out from what its record says and draws it in ink, with a grid. A settlement gets its streets, houses, walls and water from its population and its land; a building gets the rooms of an inn, a temple or a keep, storey by storey; a dungeon or a cave gets the rooms its text names, numbered in the same order.
- **Draw another** until you like it, then **Keep this plan**: it is the same every time you come back.
- **Take it away** as a picture with so many pixels to a square, for a virtual tabletop or a mapping tool, in your copy (numbers, secret doors, traps) or the players' (none of them). Or add it to the record's pictures, or make it a map in the Atlas with a hidden pin on each numbered place.
- **Write the key into the record** lists each room with its size and its ways out. Rename a room there and the plan follows.
- A plan for no record yet is under Characters → People, Shops and Places → **Places**.

## 0.9.23

- **A security fix. Please update.** In every earlier version, a web page open in the same browser as the app could send it requests: open your table to the network, or restore a backup of its own making over your world. A site set up for the purpose could also read your records. The app now answers only its own pages at its own address. It is not known to have been used against anyone.
- **Publishing a handbook is sturdier.** A repository you have only just made, with nothing in it, now works. A first publish makes a handful of requests where it made one for every file, and if GitHub asks the app to slow down, it waits and carries on.
- The security policy now says plainly what each part of the app trusts and what it does not.

## 0.9.22

- **Easier to read.** Headings, the chosen option of a few, the main buttons and small labels were a little too faint against the page; they are now clear in both themes. Links inside a sentence are underlined, not marked by colour alone.
- **Usable with the keyboard alone.** The first Tab on any screen offers **Skip to the page**. Text boxes show clearly when they have the focus. A dialog keeps Tab inside it, closes on `Esc` from anywhere in it, and hands the focus back when it closes.
- **Better with a screen reader.** Going to another screen says which screen it is, and the window's title follows. The long-text editors are called by their field's name ("Premise", "Campaign notes") and not just "Text". Buttons that show a word or a number are named by it.
- **Easier to press on a tablet.** The small diamonds on a character sheet have a finger-sized area each.
- **Reduce motion** on your device is respected: nothing slides or fades.

## 0.9.21

- **Help inside the app.** The guides are now part of the app: press **?** for help with the screen you are on, or open **Help** to read and search all of them. They work offline and match the version you have.
- **What's new**, this page, is shown after an update.
- **The desktop app remembers your preferences** (the theme, the folded menu, how you like the editor). It used to forget them each time it was opened.

## 0.9.20

- **Saving stays fast in a large library.** Saving a record took longer the more records a library had; it now takes the same time at 10,000 records as at 10.
- **The Windows and macOS apps build again.** They had not since 0.9.6.
- Every installer is now started and tried automatically before it is published.

## 0.9.19

- **Help and bug reports** (Settings): **Copy details for a bug report** gathers what is needed to fix a problem, with nothing from your world in it. You can read it before sending.
- The app keeps a small log on your computer of what went wrong.
- A screen that breaks now says so, with a way back, instead of going blank.

## 0.9.18

- **Update and restart** (Settings → Updates, in the desktop app): one click installs the new version and opens the app again. Only updates signed by this project are installed.
- Fixed: downloading a backup saved an empty file.
- Fixed: the Linux AppImage showed no pages.

## 0.9.17

- The app checks once a day whether a newer version is out, and says so in the top bar. It sends nothing about you or your worlds, and you can turn the check off.
- A guide to installing: the systems' warnings about an unsigned app, checking a download, and updating.

## 0.9.16

- The world that comes with the app has a name of its own: **Oros**. "The Worldkeeper" is the app.

## 0.9.15

- **Safe updates.** Before a new version changes how a library is stored, it makes a backup; the change happens whole or not at all; and a library that can't be opened gets a page that says what happened and what to do, instead of an app that won't start.

## 0.9.14

- **The GM app on another device**: a tablet at the table, a laptop in another room. Set a password in Settings and open it to your network when you want it.

## 0.9.13

- **Tags** (Library): rename or remove a tag everywhere.
- **Change many records at once**: tick them in a list to tag, move, reveal or archive them together.
- **Merge two records** into one, keeping every link to either.
- **Find and replace** across every record, with a look at each change first.

## 0.9.12

- **Generators**: people with a name, a look and a want, made from the names of your world's own peoples; a shop with its keeper and its stock, for the town you are in; and the day's weather for the campaign.

## 0.9.11

- **Bring your notes**: an Obsidian vault or a folder of Markdown files becomes records, with their links to each other, their tags and their pictures. You see what each note will become before anything is added.

## 0.9.10

- **Combat**: effects that end on the right turn by themselves, counters for limited uses and recharges, one initiative roll for a group, and the experience from a finished fight handed to the party.

## 0.9.9

- **A table display** for a second screen or a TV: the map as the players know it, a handout or a picture, with the turn order and the latest rolls.

## 0.9.8

- **Rolls the whole table sees.** A player rolls from their sheet and everyone sees it; you can roll behind the screen and show a roll when you choose.

## 0.9.7

- **The table hears at once.** What you change shows on the players' phones without reloading, and what they change on their sheets shows on yours.

## 0.9.6

- **The party's progress**: experience or milestones, who was at each session, and levelling a character up step by step.

## 0.9.5

- **Magic items and spells of your own**, usable everywhere the books' are.
- Fixed: the page of an item you had made yourself showed an error.

## 0.9.4

- **A stat block editor**: write a creature's whole stat block, start from one in the books, or describe a hazard.

## 0.9.3

- **Pictures on any record**, each one for you alone or for the players too.
