# The Worldkeeper 1.0.0: the beta

The Worldkeeper is an app for building a world and running D&D 2024 campaigns in it, on your own computer, with no account and no subscription. This is its beta: the features are all here, no more are being added for now, and what it needs is to be used.

If you have been waiting for a version to trust with a campaign, this is the first one that makes a promise about your data. If you are on 0.9.2 or earlier, please update: 0.9.23 fixed a security fault that every earlier version has.

## The promise

From this version on:

- **Every later version opens your libraries with everything in them.** If one ever could not, it would refuse and change nothing.
- **A copy is made first**, before any update changes how a library is stored, and an update happens whole or not at all.
- **Your backups keep working** in every later version.
- **It is tested.** Every release is checked against real libraries made by versions 0.5.0, 0.6.0, 0.9.0 and 0.9.14.

It does not promise that you can go back to an older version (restore the copy instead), or that there are no bugs. It is a beta: keep backups of your own somewhere else. The whole of it is in the [Data guide](https://github.com/n1x-shell/Worldkeeper/blob/main/docs/DATA.md#the-upgrade-promise).

## What is new since 0.9.2

**Plans of places.** Open a town, a building, a dungeon, a cave or a ship and choose **Draw a plan**. The app lays it out from what its record says.

- Draw it in **ink**, to print, or **painted**: stone, earth, timber and grass from photographs, furnishings with their shadows, torchlight, rugs, and a town with lit roofs, stalls and boats.
- **Key it**: click a number and say what is there, with encounters, traps and loot from your books.
- **Change it by hand**: rooms, passages, doors and furnishings, with ready-made pieces to set down.
- **Join plans up**: a level below, the inn inside the town, a city in districts.
- **Put it on its ground**: overgrown, shattered by a rift, aloft in the sky, adrift between worlds, or among the stars.
- Take it away as a picture that lines up with the grid of a virtual tabletop, in your copy or the players'.

**At the table.**

- What you change shows on the players' phones at once, and what they change shows on yours.
- Rolls the whole table sees, and rolls behind the screen.
- A table display for a second screen or a TV: the map as the players know it, a handout, the turn order.
- Combat that keeps track: effects that end on the right turn, limited uses, group initiative, experience handed out.
- The party's progress: experience or milestones, and levelling up step by step.
- The GM's app on another device, behind a password.

**Making things.**

- A whole stat block editor, and magic items and spells of your own.
- Creatures, hazards and items in rules packs you write as Markdown.
- Generators for people, shops and the day's weather.
- Pictures on any record, each for you alone or for the players too.

**Looking after a world.**

- Bring an Obsidian vault or a folder of Markdown notes in as records.
- Change many records at once, merge two into one, find and replace, and tidy tags.
- Help inside the app: press **?** on any screen. It works offline.

**Trust.**

- **A security fix.** Before 0.9.23, a web page open in the same browser could send the app requests, and a site made for the purpose could read your records. The app now answers only its own pages.
- Safe updates, a daily check for a newer version (it sends nothing about you; you can turn it off), and a page that says what happened if a library can't be opened.
- **Copy details for a bug report**, with nothing from your world in it.
- Clearer to read, usable with the keyboard alone, and better with a screen reader.
- Saving stays fast in a large library.

The world that comes with the app now has a name of its own, **Oros**. "The Worldkeeper" is the app.

## Installing

Installers for Windows, macOS and Linux are attached below. **They are not signed**, so Windows and macOS will warn you before opening the app. The [Install guide](https://github.com/n1x-shell/Worldkeeper/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md) says what each warning looks like and how to check your download.

If you are updating, install over the version you have. Your libraries are not touched by installing; each is updated, after its copy is made, the first time you open it.

## What has not been tried

A beta should say so.

- The installers have been started and checked automatically on all three systems. Nobody has yet installed them by hand on a Mac or a Windows PC.
- Nobody has yet run a real session at a table with this version. The checks drive browsers.
- Painted plans have not been opened in a virtual tabletop, and nobody who uses a screen reader every day has tried the app.
- It is for D&D 2024 only. There is no play over the internet: the table is the people in the room, on your own network.

## If something goes wrong

Open **Settings → Help and bug reports**, press **Copy details for a bug report**, and open an issue with what you were doing. [Troubleshooting](https://github.com/n1x-shell/Worldkeeper/blob/main/docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md) has the common faults. For a security fault, follow [the security policy](https://github.com/n1x-shell/Worldkeeper/blob/main/SECURITY.md) and do not open a public issue.

The full list of changes, version by version, is in [What's new](https://github.com/n1x-shell/Worldkeeper/blob/main/docs/WHATS_NEW.md).
