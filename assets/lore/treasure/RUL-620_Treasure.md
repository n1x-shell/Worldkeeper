# Treasure

## Magic is scarce, and it means something
The world has fewer magic items than the Dungeon Master's Guide assumes: roughly half its count, with coin, trade goods and favours making up the difference. Most of the magic a party finds is the world's own (shard-touched relics, Kaelith glyph-work, salvage from lost worlds), and each piece has a story: who made it, who lost it and who wants it back.

- **Generic items** (a +1 sword, a potion of healing) come from the Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), Xanathar's Guide to Everything and Tasha's Cauldron of Everything, and are flavoured by where they're found.
- **The world's own items** (the Threadstone, the Glass Key, the Name-thread and the rest) turn up in their own regions, and each has a record saying who wants it.
- **Artifacts** never come up on a random roll. Each rests in one place, and rumours lead to it.
- **Shards** are never treasure. They are the story.

## How much
Use the Treasure tool, which rolls by tier and region. As a guide:

| Tier | Individual treasure | A hoard | Magic items in a hoard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Levels 1 to 4 | 3d6 gp | 2d4 × 100 gp in coin and goods | 0 to 2, common or uncommon |
| Levels 5 to 10 | 2d8 × 10 gp | 8d10 × 100 gp | 1 to 2, mostly uncommon and rare |
| Levels 11 to 16 | 2d10 × 10 pp | 8d8 × 1,000 gp | 1 to 3, rare and very rare |
| Levels 17 to 20 | 2d8 × 100 pp | 6d10 × 10,000 gp | 1 to 3, very rare and legendary |

## By region
- **The Hollow Marches.** Battlefield salvage from every age: old coins of kingdoms nobody remembers, broken banners, arms from all five factions.
- **The five dominions.** Each dominion's treasure looks like its faction: Order reliquaries and blessed steel, Legion arms and trophies, Consortium devices and scrolls, Pact masks and poisons, Wildborn charms and beast-gear.
- **Kaelmar.** In the west, wealth is barter: salt, copper, seed-grafts, good tools, worth their weight in coin. Its magic is Kaelith glyph-work: ordinary 2024 magic items that look like lamp-glyphs, speaking-stones, listening bowls and glyph-lances (see Glyph-Work Spell and Item Mappings [RUL-302]). Kaelholm pays coin for anything it can catalogue.
- **The Ethereal Realm.** Salvage from lost worlds: things that were real somewhere that no longer is, drawn from Spelljammer's astral treasures among others.

## Salvage from lost worlds fades
Anything taken out of the Ethereal Realm into the world starts to fade. After a long rest in the world it is translucent, after a week it works only at night, and after a month it is gone, as if it had never been.

It can be **anchored**: someone must learn its story (where it came from, who used it) and tell it aloud where the item can hear, in a short rite of about an hour. A Veilborn can bless it instead. An anchored item is real for good. Items with no story to learn, like coins of a world nobody remembers, can't be anchored, and fade.

## Finding out what it does
An item's powers are learned during a short rest spent handling it, or with *Identify*, as usual. The world's own items also have a story, which a character learns with a DC 15 Intelligence (History or Arcana) check, or by asking the person who wants it back.
