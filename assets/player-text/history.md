# The years since the Cycle broke

## The First Morning

*Year 1 Unbound (270 AB)*

The morning after the Cycle broke, Aetheris woke and stayed as it was. Nothing reset. Men and women killed the night before were still dead at dawn, and their families waited three days for them to walk back in, the way the dead always had. They didn't.

By the end of the first week, scouts had found the other dominions sitting where nothing had been: Legion banners a day's ride from Order shrines, Wildborn forest growing over a Consortium road. Five worlds had become one, stitched at the edges. The Hollow Marches, in the middle, belonged to none of them.

**What people say**

- Old soldiers call it the Long Week: seven days when nobody knew if the world would come back the way it was.
- The dead of that first year are buried with their faces up, so they can see they've been left alone.

## The Lantern Truce

*Year 2 Unbound (271 AB)*

In the second year, the five factions needed somewhere to trade back their dead. They met at Hollow Refuge, in the old hospital that is now the Lantern House, and agreed one rule: weapons stay outside. Every envoy left a blade at the door, and every envoy walked out alive.

The rule held. Hollow Refuge has been neutral ground ever since, because every faction has needed it at least once, and none can afford to be the one that broke it.

**What people say**

- Each faction swears it called the meeting. Each faction's envoy is said to have been the last one to hand over their blade.
- Faction soldiers still leave their weapons at the Lantern House door, even when nobody's watching.

## The Border Years

*Years 3 to 8 Unbound (272 to 277 AB)*

For six years the dominions fought over where each one ended. Nobody had drawn the borders; the five worlds had simply met where they met, and every faction thought the line belonged a little further out.

The fighting settled where it settled. Each dominion's border now lies about a week from Hollow Refuge. Only on the Bloodfields, where the Legion's land meets the Wildborn's, did the fighting never stop. It's a permanent warfront to this day.

**What people say**

- Ask a Legion veteran and a Wildborn hunter who started the Bloodfields, and you'll get two answers and a fight.
- The Marches are full of border stones that were moved in the night, sometimes more than once.

## The Purging of the Halls

*Year 9 Unbound (278 AB)*

In the ninth year, a city in the Radiant Ascendancy closed its gates on the Order's tax collectors and its inquisitors. The Celestial Order answered with light. When it was over, the city was still standing, empty and white, with every name scraped off every door. It's called the Purged Halls now. Nobody lives there.

**What people say**

- Pilgrims to Empyrean Spire are walked past the Purged Halls, so they understand.
- Survivors say it was one inquisitor, and one night. The Order says it was justice.

## The Enclave Compacts

*Years 10 to 14 Unbound (279 to 283 AB)*

Once the borders held, each faction wanted a foothold inside the others. Over five years, one bargain at a time, each faction won the right to keep an enclave in every rival's dominion: a chapel, a forge-hall, a library, a den or a grove, tolerated because the other side wanted the same favour back.

The enclaves are embassies, refuges and spy-nests all at once. Most of them are all three.

**What people say**

- Every enclave was built with a back door. Every host knows where it is.
- If you need to leave a dominion quietly, the rival enclave is the first place to try.

## The Lanternless Crossing

*Year 11 Unbound (280 AB)*

In the eleventh year a smuggler ship from the Eternal Veil sailed west across the Black Tides and came back. Everyone had said there was nothing on the other side. There was a whole continent.

The crew learned the rule every sailor on the Tides still follows: put out every lantern over the deep trench in the middle of the crossing, and keep them out until it's behind you. The crew who didn't, didn't come home.

**What people say**

- The first crew brought back a passenger: someone grown, not born, with spores in their hair.
- Smugglers crossed for nearly twenty years before anyone ran a regular ship.

## The First Riftwalker

*Year 15 Unbound (284 AB)*

In 284 AB, an orc named Maro Grane climbed down into the Rift beside Ashvale and came back up three days later, cut with glyphs nobody had taught him. He started writing what he'd been told down there. Those pages became the Root Texts, and the people who gathered to read them became the Convergence Collective.

**What people say**

- He's still writing. The Collective counts its years in his chapters.
- Ashvale's Boundary Keepers watched him go down and sang until he came up again.

## The Breaking of the Text

*Year 18 Unbound (287 AB)*

In 287 AB, a copy of the Root Texts reached a mercenary legion in the eastern mountains. Its captain, Dace Corvane, read the same pages as the Collective and came to the opposite answer: the change shouldn't be offered to everyone, only to the few who could hold the rest. The Ascendancy began as a reading group in a soldiers' camp. It's still mostly that, for now.

**What people say**

- Two readings of one text, and already hatred.
- Corvane keeps a count of everything: men, coin, days. Nobody knows what the count is for.

## The Night the Stones Listened

*Year 22 Unbound (291 AB)*

On one night in 291 AB, during the keeping song, the stone circle at Ashvale listened. Everyone there felt it at once: the song going out, and something holding still to hear it. Ashvale wrote it down. It's the only time in the town's records that it's happened.

**What people say**

- The Boundary Keepers have sung more carefully ever since.
- Children born in Ashvale that year are said to hear things in the stones.

## The Burned Expedition

*Year 25 Unbound (294 AB)*

In 294 AB, Kaelholm's scholars sent their largest expedition yet into the Eastern Deepwood, to catalogue Kaelith ruins. They found the ruins, and the Spore Circle's Eastern Tendril found them. The Tendril burned every piece of Kaelith work the expedition had gathered, and sent the scholars home alive, carrying nothing.

Kaelholm's expeditions have run into the Tendril ever since.

**What people say**

- Vellum House still hires guards for every expedition, and pays better each year.
- The Tendril left a message: the old work stays burned.

## The First Glass

*Year 27 Unbound (296 AB)*

In 296 AB, shepherds in the hills found glass growing out of bare rock, smooth and warm, where there had never been any. Over the next year, seeps of it turned up in seven places across Kaelmar. Doors of glass, some of them, that open to an open hand.

**What people say**

- Whoever opens a glass door comes back with a little less of themselves.
- Nobody has found an eighth seep. People are looking.

## The Word from the Fields

*Year 30 Unbound (299 AB)*

In the thirtieth year, a gravedigger on the Bloodmarked Fields turned up earth that glowed red at night. By winter, every faction had heard the rumour: a shard lay under the Fields. By spring, all five had sent someone to Hollow Refuge, half a day's walk away.

**What people say**

- The gravedigger hasn't been seen since.
- The Crooked Nail hasn't had an empty room in months.
