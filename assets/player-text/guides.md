# Starting Guides and the Moons

## How to use this

Guides for players beginning in each of the three starting places, and the moons over both continents.

## Starting in Hollow Refuge

*A neutral town in the middle of everybody's war, where all five factions come to talk and nobody is allowed to fight.*

**What it's like.** Hollow Refuge is built from the stones of every age the Hollow Marches have lived through: a Legion gatehouse here, an Order chapel there, a wall carved with a battle nobody remembers. All five great factions need somewhere neutral, so all five leave it alone, mostly. It's full of mercenaries, scavengers, diplomats and people who'd rather not say where they're from. Weapons stay outside the Lantern House. Arguments stay outside the Crooked Nail, or Mara Venn puts you out with them.

**People you might know**

- @[Mara Venn], who runs the Crooked Nail, the inn and mercenary exchange where most jobs start.
- @[Old Vara], the smith, who prefers favours to coin.
- @[Illa Reed], a trader who'll buy anything rare enough to sell twice.
- @[Brann Kest], a scarred veteran who doesn't sleep much.
- @[Sister Elyra Vale], a Celestial Order healer who works the Marches.
- The Blind Seer, in his windowless tower, who sees more than he says.

**Good backgrounds.** Refuge Scavenger, Shardwar Orphan, Aetherfall Survivor, Legion Deserter, Mercenary and Cult Escapee all have a reason to be here. So does anyone running from one of the five factions.

**Good callings.** Any. Warriors, rogues and rangers find work at the Crooked Nail; mages and mystics come for the Market of Lost Things; paladins and shamans come because the Marches need them.

**Why you're here (roll or choose)**

| d6 | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | You owe Mara Venn money, or a favour, and she's calling it in. |
| 2 | You deserted from a faction army and the Refuge is the one place they can't follow. |
| 3 | You came to sell something you found on the Bloodmarked Fields. Nobody will tell you what it's worth. |
| 4 | Someone you love went into the Marches a year ago. The trail ends here. |
| 5 | You had a dream about this town before you'd ever heard of it. |
| 6 | You were born here, and you've never been able to leave for long. |

**Questions to answer with your GM**

- Which of the five factions has wronged you, and which one do you secretly admire?
- What did you lose in the last war?
- Who in the Refuge would hide you if you needed it?

**What people say**

- Nobody fights in the Refuge. Nobody who wants to come back, anyway.
- Everyone ends up at the Crooked Nail eventually.
- The Old Wall has a battle carved on it that never happened. Yet.

## Starting in Ashvale

*A young town on the edge of the Rift, where everyone sings the same old song and nobody quite remembers why.*

**What it's like.** Ashvale is three hundred years old and still half-built: a meeting hall that's never been finished, a grafted orchard taking root, a ring of thirteen standing stones where the keeping song is sung. Three groups run the town and agree on almost nothing except that the song must be kept: the Boundary Keepers, who sing it; the Bloom-Speakers, who listen to the Bloom; and the Wrights, who make things that work. Nine villages lie in the hills around it, and it's a long way from anywhere else.

**People you might know**

- @[Ritekeeper Orla Hesk], who keeps the rite, and @[Keeper Jory Hesk], her grandson, who teaches the song to the children.
- @[Master Speaker Tevan Lin], who teaches communion with the Bloom to anyone who asks.
- @[Chief Wright Brenna Halvor], who is planting the orchard and always needs salvage.
- @[Odran Reeve], who trades salt up from Low Fenn.

**Good backgrounds.** Stone-Singer, Graft-Keeper, Circle Initiate, Camp Healer and Burned Scholar all fit Ashvale. So does a child of any of the nine villages.

**Good callings.** Bards and clerics of Sephane keep the song; druids and rangers walk with the Bloom-Speakers; artificers, fighters and rogues work with the Wrights.

**Why you're here (roll or choose)**

| d6 | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | You were born here, and you know every verse of the song. |
| 2 | You came from Kaelholm with your name burned off the rolls, and Ashvale didn't ask. |
| 3 | You work for Brenna Halvor, and the salvage under the west road is calling. |
| 4 | You came to learn communion from Tevan Lin, and stayed. |
| 5 | Your village sent you with a message nobody in Ashvale wants to hear. |
| 6 | You dreamed of thirteen stones before you ever saw them. |

**Questions to answer with your GM**

- Which of the three groups do you side with, and which one do you argue with?
- What do you think the keeping song is for?
- Who in the nine villages would you go back for?

**What people say**

- Everyone who sleeps in Ashvale is a hand on the key. Nobody says what key.
- There's always a Hesk at the stones.
- Something hums under the west road at night.

## Starting in the Nameless Asylum

*A refuge in the grey border of the Ethereal Realm, for people who remember too much.*

**What it's like.** The Nameless Asylum stands on the grey grass of the Hollow Verge, an old house with too many rooms and a lamp at every door. Everyone here slipped out of the world somehow: out of an Aetherfall, off a root-road, through a door that wasn't there. Some remember other lives; some remember nothing. Weapons stay sheathed, names are spoken carefully, and every evening the keeper counts everyone in, so that nobody is forgotten.

**People you might know**

- @[Keeper Lantern], the warforged keeper, who counts everyone in.
- @[Sister Nobody], the healer, whose face is a little different for everyone.
- @[Tessk the Wayfinder], a githyanki guide to the Verge's paths.
- @[Brindle Quickfoot], a harengon who trades salvage from the edge of the Fields.
- @[Inkling], a young kenku who copies down every life told in the Remembering Rooms.

**Good backgrounds.** Slipped the Cycle, Asylum Ward, Fields Salvager, Colosseum Echo and Root-road Stray are made for the Asylum.

**Good callings.** Any, but the realm rewards some: clerics of Sephane and monks keep names steady, warlocks and sorcerers hear things here, and rangers and rogues know the grey paths.

**Why you're here (roll or choose)**

| d6 | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | You woke on the grey grass with no idea how you got there. |
| 2 | You remember a world that doesn't exist any more, and the world couldn't bear you remembering it. |
| 3 | You came by root-road from Kaelmar, and the road didn't take you back. |
| 4 | You were born here, and you've never seen the sun. |
| 5 | Someone you came with crossed the last threshold. You didn't. |
| 6 | You're looking for your own name. Someone here must know it. |

**Questions to answer with your GM**

- What do you remember from before, and what do you wish you didn't?
- Who at the Asylum would notice if you didn't come back one evening?
- What would you give to go home, and is there a home to go to?

**What people say**

- Everyone at the Asylum is someone. Nobody's sure who.
- Every evening the list of names is longer.
- The Verge finds you when you need it. It doesn't let you leave when you want.

## The Moons

*Two moons over both continents: the white Lamp, and the small red Wanderer that keeps its own time.*

Two moons cross the sky over Aetheris and Kaelmar. **The Lamp** is large and white, and it keeps nearly in step with the months, slipping a few days each year. Sailors and farmers count by it. **The Wanderer** is small and red, and it keeps time with nothing: it comes round every seven weeks, so it drifts against the months and the Lamp, and the two meet full only now and then.

In Aetheris, the Lamp belongs to Solenne, and the Wanderer to nobody. In Kaelmar, they call the Wanderer the Ember, and say it was there before the Bloom.

**What people say**

- When both moons are full together, it's a good night for a wedding and a bad night for a secret.
- When both moons are dark, stay indoors. Doors open on nights like that that shouldn't.
- The Wanderer isn't a moon at all. Nobody agrees what it is instead.
- Sailors on the Black Tides navigate by the Lamp and swear at the Wanderer.
