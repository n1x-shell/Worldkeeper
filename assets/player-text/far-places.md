# Far places, the sea and the long roads

## How to use this

The far corners of Kaelmar, the islands of the Black Tides, the song Ashvale sings, and how far and how long the world's journeys run. Each section becomes a handbook page.

## The Keeping Song

*The four lines every child in Ashvale learns, sung at the thirteen stones.*

> *Thirteen stand and one lies under,*
> *hush the root and hold the thunder.*
> *Every hand upon the key:*
> *never thirteen, never three.*

Everyone in Ashvale knows the words. Nobody agrees what they mean. The Boundary Keepers sing it at the stones every night. Children skip to it. Old people hum it when they can't sleep.

**At the table.** If your character has slept in Ashvale, singing the song at the stones counts as hearing your true name: once per day, you have Advantage on a Thinning save.

**What people say**

- "One lies under" is the founder, buried beneath the stones. Or it isn't.
- The thunder is the Rift. Everyone knows that. Everyone says something different about it.
- Never thirteen, never three: it's about counting sheep. Ask a Hesk and watch their face.

## Distances in Aetheris

*How far is a week's walk?*

Aetheris measures journeys in days, not miles: half a day to the Bloodmarked Fields, a week to a dominion's border. If you need miles, a day's travel on a road is about 24 miles (40 km), and less in the Hollow Marches, where the roads give out.

- **The Hollow Marches** are about four days across, roughly 100 miles.
- **Each dominion's border** is about a week from Hollow Refuge, roughly 170 miles.
- **Each capital** is about another week beyond its border, roughly 340 miles from the Refuge.
- **Aetheris** is about 750 miles across, much the same size as Kaelmar.

The map shows where places are, not how far apart. Trust the travellers' times on the routes.

## Crossing the Black Tides

*Two weeks in the dark, and what it costs.*

The Black Tides are about 700 miles wide, from the ports of the Eternal Veil to Kaelholm. Ships cross slowly, under short sail, with every lantern out over the trench. It takes about two weeks.

**Passage.** About 70 gp for the crossing, with food. Smugglers charge double and ask no questions. You can work your passage as crew, if a captain will have you.

**The dark toll.** The Shadow Pact takes one coin in ten on every cargo at @[Tollrock], both ways. Anything undeclared that the Pact finds is forfeit. Everyone undeclares something.

**Kaelholm's catalogue.** Kaelith salvage landed in Kaelmar must be shown at Kaelholm's catalogue office, in the city or on @[Kittiwake]. Kaelholm can buy anything it likes at a fair price. Salvage that isn't shown is contraband.

**What's worth carrying.** Goods from the other continent sell for double the usual price, the first time they're sold across the sea.

- **To Kaelmar:** Legion steel, Order relic-oil, Consortium paper and inks, Veil masks and lacquer, Wildborn furs, wine.
- **To Aetheris:** graftwood, living cord, glow-fungus (packed dark for the crossing), glyph-ink, Low Fenn salt, and any Kaelith salvage Kaelholm let go.

**Barter in western Kaelmar.** West of the big towns, coin is scarce. A sack of Low Fenn salt is worth a gold piece. A day's work on the orchard or the walls is worth 2 silver. A working Kaelith part is worth whatever the Wrights will give for it.

## Journeys in the Ethereal Realm

*In the realm, how far you go depends on who you go with.*

The Ethereal Realm doesn't measure journeys by distance. Two places are as near as the travellers' shared purpose makes them.

- **The Hollow Verge.** Each leg between known places, such as the Nameless Asylum, the Rift's mouth or a threshold, takes 1d4 days. A guide helps: roll twice and take the lower. Without one, add a day, because the paths move.
- **Through the Rift**, from one continent's open end to the other's, takes 2d6 days.
- **Root-roads** take minutes, and cost a Thinning save.
- **Past the last threshold**, a journey to any region takes 1d6 days. If the party travels with one shared purpose, said aloud each morning, it takes 1d3. If they quarrel, add 1d6.
- **The Black Horizon** can't be reached by walking, however long you walk.

**What people say**

- Say why you're going every morning, out loud. The Verge listens.
- Two people walked to the same place on the same day. One arrived a week before the other.

## Tollrock

*The last light before the dark.*

> A bare grey rock a day out from the coast, with a stone customs house, a cistern and a lighthouse. At dusk the keeper climbs the tower and puts the light out, and every ship at anchor goes quiet.

**What you know**

- The Shadow Pact's customs post. Every ship crossing the Black Tides stops here, both ways, and pays the dark toll.
- It's the last light ships see before the dark, and the first they see coming home.

**What people say**

- The Pact's clerks never ask what's in a crate. They already know.
- Nobody's ever seen who lights the lighthouse in the morning.

## The Kneelers

*Black stacks at the edge of the trench, bowed toward the deep.*

> A ring of black rock stands out of the swell, each stack bent like a figure kneeling toward the open sea. Masts lean among them, and the hulls of ships that came too close. The sailors stop talking until the Kneelers are out of sight.

**What you know**

- They stand at the western edge of the trench, where the sea goes dark.
- Ships give them a wide berth. Wrecks lie all round them.

**What people say**

- The Drowned Saints pray there, on nights with no moon.
- Count the Kneelers going out and coming back. If the number's changed, don't say so.

## Kittiwake

*A green island of fishers and gulls, off the Kaelmar coast.*

> Wind, grass and gulls, and a harbour full of fishing boats with patched brown sails. Above the quay, a slate-roofed office flies Kaelholm's banner, and a clerk sits outside it with a ledger, watching every boat come in.

**What you know**

- Two days off the Kaelmar coast, below Kaelholm.
- Tidecant was born here, when Aetheris smugglers and Kaelish fishers first had to bargain.
- Kaelholm keeps a catalogue office on the quay.

**What people say**

- Every Kittiwake child speaks three languages, and two of them are Tidecant.
- The catalogue clerk has never once refused a drink.

## Hearthdeep

*A dwarf hold cut deep into the northern highlands.*

> The road climbs to a door in the mountain, iron-bound and twice the height of a goliath. Inside, warm air rises from hot springs, forges glow down every hall, and the walls are carved with thirteen keys.

**What you know**

- The dwarf hold of the northern highlands, built over @[Anvilreach], one of the thirteen sealed vaults.
- @[Hold-Warden Brisa Deepmantle] keeps it.
- It trades iron south for food.

**What people say**

- The deeper halls are warm in midwinter. The dwarves won't say why.
- The Hold-Warden carries thirteen keys and has never used one.

## Whitecairn

*The last shelter before the ice.*

> A low stone hut and a tall cairn of white stones, on a ridge where the heather gives out and the ice begins. Smoke comes from the chimney. Behind the hut, a row of smaller cairns runs off into the snow.

**What you know**

- A way-station at the edge of the ice, the last shelter on the road toward @[Rimeholt].
- @[Kaunna] keeps it.

**What people say**

- Kaunna can tell you the weather three days ahead, and she's never wrong.
- Every small cairn behind the hut is someone who didn't listen to her.

## Tumblewater

*A halfling river-market where three rivers meet.*

> Rafts, barges and pilings, lashed together into a town that rocks gently underfoot. Bells ring from every jetty. Halflings shout prices across the water, and a stout woman with a ledger and a pipe seems to be shouting at all of them at once.

**What you know**

- A river-market on rafts and pilings, where three rivers of the southern country meet.
- It moves a little every year. Everything on the waterways passes through it.
- @[Harbour-mother Hettie Goodbarrel] runs it.

**What people say**

- Tumblewater has been in four places in living memory. Nobody remembers it moving.
- If it floats, Hettie knows its name.

## Mossback

*A fen village on the backs of sleeping turtles.*

> In the deep fen, the reeds open on a village of huts and moss, and half of it stands on great shell-backed islands that breathe, very slowly. A tortle older than anything around him sits on the biggest one, and nods to you, eventually.

**What you know**

- A tortle and lizardfolk village in the deep fen. Half of it is built on the backs of island-turtles that never move.
- It's the fens' meeting ground. @[Elder Gorrum] is its elder.

**What people say**

- The island-turtles are asleep. If they ever wake, Mossback goes with them.
- Ask Gorrum a question and come back tomorrow for the answer.

## Ashgather

*The orc herders' gathering ground, in the ash barrens.*

> Twice a year the empty grey plain fills up overnight: tents, smoke, a thousand head of shaggy cattle, drums, and voices raised in trade and argument. In the middle, under a tall pole hung with bells, the speaker listens to everyone.

**What you know**

- A seasonal tent-town where the orc herders of the ash barrens meet, twice a year.
- It's a market, a court and a wedding fair.
- @[Speaker Ghora Coalsong] settles its disputes.

**What people say**

- Every bell on the speaker's staff is a judgement. Some of them have never stopped ringing.
- Half the weddings at Ashgather are made to end a feud. The other half start one.

## Cinderwell

*A warm spring in the ash barrens.*

> A pool of clear, steaming water in the middle of grey nothing, ringed by a few stone huts and the hoofprints of a thousand cattle. The ground is warm under your boots, even at night.

**What you know**

- A hot spring in the ash barrens, where the herders water their beasts.
- It's warm all year, in the coldest winter.

**What people say**

- Something under the ash keeps the water warm. The herders call it "the old fire" and leave it alone.
- Nobody owns Cinderwell. Nobody fights there.

## Kaunna

*Keeps Whitecairn, the last shelter before the ice.*

**Looks.** A grey-skinned goliath in furs rimed with frost, with a shovel always to hand.

**Manner.** Few words, all of them about the weather. Kind, in a way that doesn't look like it.

**Who they are**

- Keeps the way-station at the edge of the ice, on the road toward Rimeholt.
- Came north from the Eastern Mountains, and never went back.

**What people say**

- She reads the weather and buries the ones who didn't.
- Ask her why she left the mountains and she'll tell you it's going to snow.

## Elder Gorrum

*The very old elder of Mossback.*

| People | Class | Known as | Pronouns | From |

| Tortle | Druid | Shaman | He/him | @[Mossback] |

**Looks.** A very old tortle with a moss-grown shell and eyes like wet stones.

**Manner.** Slow, patient and unhurried by anything. Answers questions a day after they're asked.

**Who they are**

- Elder of Mossback, the fens' meeting ground.

**What people say**

- Gorrum is older than the island-turtles. The turtles think so too.
- His answers are always right, and always a day late.

**Looks.** A broad orc with ash-grey braids and a herder's staff hung with bells, one for every judgement.

**Manner.** Listens to everyone, to the end. Then rings a bell, and that's that.

- Speaker of Ashgather, who hears the herders' disputes when the herds meet.

- Ghora's never given a judgement twice. Nobody's ever asked them to.
