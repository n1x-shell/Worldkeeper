# Session Zero

## How to use this

Everything a table needs for its first evening together, before anyone rolls a die: a one-page primer for each starting place, the world's house rules in plain words, and the safety tools the table agrees to use. Read your starting place's primer, then the house rules, then talk about safety together.

## Session Zero

*The evening before the first adventure: make characters, agree on the game, and meet each other.*

**What this world asks of you**

This world works best when the table agrees to four things before anyone makes a character.

1. **Losing memories and names is part of play.** Some places and powers wear away at who you are (see House Rules). Write down a few named memories and the people who know your true name, and when you lose one, play it as part of your character's story, not as a punishment.
2. **Not every fight should be fought, and not every question gets answered.** Nothing resets, and some deaths are final. Running, surrendering and talking are real choices. Some of the world's mysteries stay mysteries, and what people say is what they believe, not always the truth.
3. **The party sticks together and knows each other's names.** In this world, friends who call you by your name can pull you back. Characters who keep secrets from the party, or go off alone, lose the best protection there is.
4. **The tone is dark and sad, and the table's lines and veils are respected.** The stories here are about forgetting, grief and being forgotten. Agree on what's off-limits before you start (see Safety Tools), and use the pause whenever you need it.

If that sounds like your kind of game, you'll get the most out of this world. If any of it doesn't, say so now, so the GM can adjust.

**What happens at session zero**

1. The GM says which starting place the campaign begins in, and everyone reads its primer.
2. The table agrees on the safety tools (see Safety Tools), and everyone names their lines and veils.
3. Everyone makes a character together, using the guided builder or the class pages. Pick a background from the starting place's guide if you can.
4. Each player answers their starting place's "Questions to answer with your GM", out loud if they're happy to.
5. The party works out how they know each other (see below).
6. The GM explains the house rules (see House Rules), and the table asks questions.

**How do you know each other? (go round the table)**

Each player, in turn, picks someone already at the table and answers one of these about them:

- Which of us saved your life, and do they know it?
- Which of us owes you money, or a favour?
- Which of us do you trust with your true name?
- Which of us did you meet at the worst moment of your life?
- Which of us do you not quite trust yet, and why?
- Which of us reminds you of someone you lost?

**Things to agree as a table**

- How deadly should it feel? The world can be very dangerous; ask the GM how often fights are meant to be won by running.
- How much do you want to talk, fight and explore? Most tables want all three; say which you'd miss most.
- How are absences handled? Is a missing player's character quietly elsewhere, or played by someone else?
- Does the party stay together? This world rewards it: people who say each other's names come back.

## Hollow Refuge in One Page

*Everything your character knows before the first session, on one page.*

**The world in a breath.** Thirty-one years ago, the Cycle broke. Before that, Aetheris reset itself at every Aetherfall, and the dead came back. Now nothing resets, the dead stay dead, and five worlds have become one, stitched together at the edges. It is Year 31 Unbound.

**Where you are.** Hollow Refuge, the neutral town at the heart of the Hollow Marches, where all five great factions come to talk and nobody is allowed to fight. Weapons stay outside the Lantern House.

**The five great factions, in a line each.** Each rules a dominion that borders the Marches.

- **The Crimson Legion**, the war-host of the Tyrant's Dominion: only the strong deserve to rule.
- **The Celestial Order**, of the Radiant Ascendancy: divine law, purity, and a sun that never sets.
- **The Wildborn Clans**, of the Verdant Rebirth: civilisation is a disease, and the forest is the cure.
- **The Arcane Consortium**, of the Fractured Nexus: reality is a puzzle, and they mean to solve it.
- **The Shadow Pact**, masked rulers of the Eternal Veil: truth is whatever is believed.

**What everyone here knows.**

- The Bloodmarked Fields, half a day away, are a war over shards that never stops.
- The Rift, at the far edge of the Marches, has no bottom. Few who go down it come back.
- Shards are pieces of what broke the Cycle. Touching one marks you for life.
- Across the Black Tides lies Kaelmar, a second continent. The crossing is made in the dark.

**What you don't know yet.** Why the Cycle broke. Who's really in charge of the Refuge. What the Blind Seer sees.

**The feeling of it.** A frontier town after the end of the world. Deals in back rooms, scars everyone has, a war just over the hill, and the sense that you're living in the first chapter of something.

**Bring to the table.** A reason to be in the Refuge, a faction you hate, a faction you secretly admire, and someone you lost in the last war.

## Ashvale in One Page

*Everything your character knows before the first session, on one page.*

**The world in a breath.** Three hundred years ago, Kaelith ended in the Shrouding, and Kaelmar has been picking up its pieces ever since: glass doors, root-roads, iron things that still walk. Across the Black Tides lies Aetheris, where something called the Cycle broke thirty-one years ago. Ships cross the Black Tides in the dark, but few in Ashvale have made the crossing.

**Where you are.** Ashvale, a young, half-built town on the edge of the Rift, ringed by thirteen standing stones and nine villages in the hills. It's a long way from anywhere else.

**The three groups that run the town, in a line each.**

- **The Boundary Keepers** sing the keeping song at the stones, every night.
- **The Bloom-Speakers** listen to the Bloom, the living roots under Kaelmar.
- **The Wrights** salvage Kaelith's old works and make things that work.

**What everyone here knows.**

- The keeping song must be sung. Everyone agrees on that, and nobody agrees on why.
- Glass doors and root-roads are quick and dangerous. They wear away at who you are.
- Under the west road, old Kaelith tunnels run east, and something hums down there at night.
- Kaelholm, the big city, burns people's names off its rolls. Ashvale doesn't ask.

**What you don't know yet.** What the song is for. What the key is. Why the stones listen.

**The feeling of it.** A small, stubborn town on the edge of the unknown, with old ruins underfoot, a song nobody understands, and neighbours who'll argue with you all day and bury you properly when it's over.

**Bring to the table.** A group you side with, a group you argue with, a guess about what the song is for, and someone in the villages you'd go back for.

## The Nameless Asylum in One Page

*Everything your character knows before the first session, on one page.*

**The world in a breath.** The world has two continents, Aetheris and Kaelmar, and beneath both lies the Ethereal Realm, where things the world forgets end up. You're there now, on its edge.

**Where you are.** The Nameless Asylum, an old house with too many rooms and a lamp at every door, on the grey grass of the Hollow Verge. Everyone here slipped out of the world somehow. Every evening, the keeper counts everyone in, so that nobody is forgotten.

**What everyone here knows.**

- In the Verge, you can die like anywhere else. Past the last threshold, death is final, and worse: the dead are erased, and the world forgets them.
- Names matter. Say your friends' names out loud every day. Don't say names carelessly in the grass.
- Crossing the last threshold brings back every life you've ever lived, all at once.
- There are ways back into the world, through the Rift and the root-roads. They're not easy, and they don't always go where you want.

**What you don't know yet.** Why you're here, maybe. Who Sister Nobody really is. Whether there's a home to go back to.

**The feeling of it.** A quiet, strange, kind place, full of people who don't quite know who they are, holding on to each other in the grey. Gentle, eerie and sad, with danger just outside the door.

**Bring to the table.** Something you remember from before, something you wish you didn't, and a name: yours, or the one you're looking for.

## House Rules

*The world's own rules, in plain words.*

These are the rules this world adds to the books. Your GM has the full text; this is what they mean for you.

**Thinning.** Some things wear away at who you are: opening a glass door, riding a root-road, communing too deeply with the Bloom, trading with a memory-house. When one does, you make a Charisma saving throw. The number to beat is 12 plus your Thinning (which starts at 0).

- If you fail, your Thinning goes up by one, and you cross out one named memory in your character's notes, for good. So write some down: a mother's face, the smell of your village, the name of your first horse.
- At Thinning 3, you stop answering to your name without effort.
- At Thinning 6, you're gone: your character joins the harmony, and becomes the GM's.
- Your friends can pull you back. If someone who knows your true name says it to you, you get Advantage on the save. A month among people who call you by your name takes Thinning down by one. Lost memories don't come back.
- **Anchors.** A friend who knows your true name can take a Thinning save for you, at the same number. If they succeed, you're both safe. If they fail, the Thinning is theirs: their Thinning goes up, and they cross out one of their own memories. Each person can be anchored once a day.
- Keep a list of the people who know your true name. It's the most useful thing on your sheet.

**Shardscarred.** If your character has touched a shard, they're marked for life, and they're Shardscarred: you have Advantage on saving throws against anything that changes your memory or rewrites reality. Ask your GM before choosing it, because every faction wants people like you, for its own reasons.

**Unmaking.** In the deep Ethereal Realm, past the last threshold, death is final: no spell, no relic and no wish brings anyone back. Worse, the world forgets them: their statues change, and their families remember someone else. Death saving throws work as usual until then. If a character is Unmade, every surviving companion writes one true sentence about them. It changes nothing in the rules, and everything at the table.

**Things you can't kill.** Some things in this world can't be beaten by doing damage. Swords pass through them and spells slide off. They're never stopped by running out of hit points; they're stopped by doing something: breaking what holds them here, closing the way they came in, or getting there in time. If your GM says something can't be harmed, believe them, and start looking for the other way.

**Some things you shouldn't look at.** If your GM warns you that something is too much to look at, your character can look away. If you look anyway, you make a Wisdom saving throw, and a failure can leave a mark on your mind that lasts a long time.

## Safety Tools

*How the table keeps the game fun for everyone, even when the story gets dark.*

This world has dark corners: people losing their memories and their names, being forgotten by everyone who loved them, bodies taken over by something else, war, cults and grief. That's part of what makes it work. These tools make sure the dark stays the kind everyone signed up for.

**Lines and veils.** At session zero, everyone names their lines and veils, privately to the GM if they'd rather.

- A **line** is something that won't happen in the game at all.
- A **veil** is something that can happen, but off-screen: the story cuts away, and picks up after.

Things worth thinking about in this world: losing a loved one's memory of you, possession, body horror, harm to children, torture, insects and crawling things, drowning and the dark, and real-world grief that a story about forgetting might touch.

**The pause.** Anyone can say "pause" (or tap the table, or hold up a card) at any time, for any reason, and nobody asks why. The scene stops. The table changes it, skips it, or takes a break.

**The open door.** Anyone can step away from the table at any time, and they don't need to explain. When they come back, they're welcome.

**Check-ins.** After a heavy scene, the GM asks how everyone is doing. At the end of every session, each player says one thing they liked, and one thing they'd like more of, or less.

**Your character, your call.** Nothing changes who your character is without you. If a possession, a Thinning or an Unmaking would take your character somewhere you don't want to go, say so, and the table finds another way.
