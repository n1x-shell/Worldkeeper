# Player Adventure Book: Peoples, Callings, Powers and Customs

## How to use this

Lore text for the peoples, callings, factions, magic and customs of the world. Each starts with a one-line blurb. Peoples and callings end with a short "At the table" note for building a character.

## Humans

*Adaptable survivors, found everywhere and like nobody else.*

Humans are found in every dominion, every city and every ruin on both continents, and no two human communities are alike. They learn fast and fit any role, though they have no gifts of their own to lean on. In Kaelmar they are the most numerous people.

**Strengths and flaws.** Quick to learn and adapt. No innate gifts.

**Common names.** Alden, Mara, Kest, Lysa, Orin, Elyra, Vaal, Reed

**At the table.** Use the human rules.

## Elves

*Ancient, proud and long-remembering, attuned to magic and the wild.*

Elves are graceful casters and archers with long memories. They are found in Consortium academies, Wildborn enclaves and the communities that live among the ruins. In Kaelmar, the Spore Circle's oldest elves saw the Shrouding with their own eyes, three hundred years ago.

**Strengths and flaws.** Graceful casters and archers. Proud and distant.

**Culture.** Scholarship, artistry and long memory.

**Common names.** Avel, Iria, Leth, Nael, Oris, Sera, Therin, Vale

**At the table.** Use the elf rules.

## Dwarves

*Stoneborn mountain folk: enduring, stubborn and keepers of records.*

Dwarves are enduring warriors and master crafters, and they keep records of everything. Their records do not always agree with the world. In Kaelmar, dwarves were Kaelith's masons, and they still hold the northern highlands.

**Strengths and flaws.** Enduring warriors and master crafters. Stubborn.

**Culture.** Engineering, stonecraft and record-keeping.

**Common names.** Brom, Dara, Harn, Kella, Orra, Vara, Venn, Yorr

**At the table.** Use the dwarf rules.

## Orcs

*Warborn, loyal and strong, and very often not Legion.*

Orcs have unmatched strength and a famous battle rage, and they value loyalty and honour among their own. They live in military clans, merchant houses and monastic communities, and many emphatically do not belong in the Crimson Legion. In Kaelmar they herd the ash barrens.

**Strengths and flaws.** Unmatched strength and battle rage. Loyal and honourable among their own. Impatient.

**Common names.** Drak, Hessa, Kor, Maraak, Ruun, Torga, Varek, Zhar

**At the table.** Use the orc rules.

## Reptilians

*Scaled hunters of swamp, desert and jungle, patient and hard to read.*

Reptilians, also called lizardfolk, are scaled hunters of swamp, desert and jungle. They are tough ambush predators who value observation, patience, memory and ritual. Others find them cold and hard to read. In Kaelmar, lizardfolk keep the southern fens.

**Strengths and flaws.** Tough ambush predators. Cold and hard to read.

**Common names.** Issik, Karra, Reth, Saal, Serek, Tass, Vesh, Zikka

**At the table.** Use the lizardfolk rules, or dragonborn rules described as scaled hunters.

## Bloomborn

*People of bark, moss, root and fungus, grown from the first years of the Bloom.*

In the first years of the Bloom, the spore-storms rewrote some people into beast-folk. A few they rewrote further, all the way into the Bloom's own grammar. Their descendants are the Bloomborn: people of bark, moss, root and fungus.

They are people, not the Bloom's moods. They have names, families, grudges and opinions, and they choose for themselves.

**Strengths and flaws.** Patient, hardy and hard to kill. Natural growers and healers. Slow to trust, slower to forget, and they always smell faintly of rain on soil.

**Culture.** Most Bloomborn live in the open Bloom country and the Eastern Deepwood. Many walk with the Spore Circle. Some work Ashvale's orchards beside the Wrights. Kaelholm studies them with fascination and suspicion. The Convergence Collective calls them proof that the change can be lived. Plenty of ordinary folk just find them restful company.

Bloomborn grow no husk against the Bloom, since they are already part of it. They feel its moods the way others feel weather. A very few have crossed to Aetheris, where nobody knows what to make of them.

**Common names.** Alder, Bracken, Corrin, Fennick, Hollin, Rue, Sedge, Tansy, Thistle, Wren

**At the table.** Use the firbolg rules, or a custom lineage.

## Warrior

*The Iron Vanguard: heavy armour, blocks, parries and brute force.*

Warriors are the Iron Vanguard. They fight in heavy armour and win with blocks, parries and brute force. Every army, mercenary company and caravan guard in the world is built around them.

**At the table.** A fighter, usually a Champion or a Battle Master.

## Rogue

*The Silent Blade: stealth, twin weapons, poison and the perfect strike.*

The Silent Blades work by stealth, with twin weapons, poison and a single perfect strike. The Shadow Pact has the most famous of them, but every faction employs a few.

**At the table.** A rogue, usually an Assassin or a Thief.

## Mage

*The Arcane Scholar: elemental destruction at range, and fragile up close.*

Mages are the Arcane Scholars. They destroy with the elements, at range, and are fragile when anything reaches them. The Arcane Consortium trains more of them than anyone.

**At the table.** A wizard (often an Evoker) or a sorcerer.

## Ranger

*The Hunter of Beasts: bows, traps, tracking and animal companions.*

Rangers are the Hunters of Beasts. They work with bows and traps, track anything that leaves a trail, and often travel with an animal companion. The Wildborn hold them in high honour.

**At the table.** A ranger, usually a Beast Master or a Hunter.

## Paladin

*The Divine Champion: a holy warrior who protects and heals.*

Paladins are the Divine Champions: holy warriors sworn to protect and to heal. The Celestial Order's knights are the most famous, but not every paladin serves the Order, and some it has cast out.

**At the table.** A paladin, usually of the Oath of Devotion or of Vengeance.

## Necromancer

*The Harbinger of Death: undead servants, stolen life and curses.*

Necromancers are the Harbingers of Death. They command undead servants, drain life and lay curses. Most people fear them. Some factions find them very useful.

**At the table.** A wizard who favours necromancy spells.

## Spellblade

*The Arcane Swordsman: blade and spell together.*

Spellblades are the Arcane Swordsmen. They enchant their weapons and fight with blade and spell together. The arenas of Varkhaal love them.

**At the table.** A fighter who is an Eldritch Knight.

## Monk

*The Way of the Fist: unarmed speed, breath and inner power.*

Monks follow the Way of the Fist: unarmed speed, breath control and inner energy. Monasteries of every faith and none train them.

**At the table.** A monk, usually a Warrior of the Open Hand.

## Shaman

*The Spiritwalker: ancestral guardians, elemental forces and visions.*

Shamans are the Spiritwalkers. They call on ancestral guardians and elemental forces, and they see visions. The Wildborn and the Taur'n honour them above most.

**At the table.** A druid, usually of the Circle of the Land or of the Moon.

## Mystic

*The Fatebinder: divination, bending chance and magic of the mind.*

Mystics are the Fatebinders. They read what is coming, bend chance, and work magic on the mind. People seek them out, and are careful what they ask.

**At the table.** A wizard (Diviner), a sorcerer (Clockwork Sorcery) or a bard (College of Lore).

## Kaelholm (Faction)

*Kaelmar's strongest power: grey-robed archivists who want the change turned back.*

**What they want.** The change turned back, and the world sorted "into its drawers". Every old vault opened and catalogued.

**How they work.** A scholar corps, monasteries, archives, and tribunals of elders in grey. Kaelholm "burns" offenders by scraping their names off every roll.

**Who leads.** Archivist-Elder @[Venn Tull|NPC-308].

**Who opposes them.** The Spore Circle's Eastern Tendril, which burns Kaelith work on sight.

## The Spore Circle

*Druids of the Bloom, who tend the conversation between people and the Bloom.*

**What they want.** To tend the conversation between people and the Bloom.

**How they work.** Druids bound by communion, ranked in Tendrils. The Sixth Tendril goes deepest. The Eastern Tendril burns Kaelith work and carries lamps grown in gourds.

**Who leads.** Many elders. Its oldest elves saw the Shrouding as children. @[Elder Sorrel] leads the Eastern Tendril, from @[Thornhaven].

**Who opposes them.** Kaelholm, whose vault expeditions the Eastern Tendril fights.

## The Convergence Collective

*A young faith that wants the change finished, but only for those who choose it.*

**What they want.** The change finished as it was first intended: chosen, and never forced.

**How they work.** A faith with Root Texts, elders and "cycles". Its Warband rides half-living constructs. Members are cut with glyphs and grafts.

**Who leads.** Its first Riftwalker, @[Maro Grane], who is still writing the Root Texts.

**Who opposes them.** The Ascendancy, whose ladder of rulers they reject.

## The Ascendancy

*A mercenary doctrine that wants a few minds raised high and everyone else beneath them.*

**What they want.** A ladder: a few minds raised through the change, and the rest held beneath them.

**How they work.** Drilled columns, siege constructs, and soldiers remade with iron and graft. For now it is a doctrine inside a mercenary legion in the eastern mountains, not yet an army.

**Who leads.** @[Marshal Dace Corvane].

**Who opposes them.** The Convergence Collective, which insists the change must be freely chosen.

## Boundary Keepers

*Ashvale's keepers of the rite, who sing the keeping song at the stones.*

**What they want.** The old rites kept, and the lid held.

**How they work.** They sing the keeping song at Ashvale's standing stones, and make sure it is sung right.

**Who leads.** @[Ritekeeper Orla Hesk].

**Who they argue with.** The Bloom-Speakers, who want to teach what the Keepers would rather keep closed. Ashvale's three groups agree on the rite and little else.

## Bloom-Speakers

*Ashvale's teachers of safe communion with the Bloom.*

**What they want.** Communion with the Bloom, taught safely, to anyone who asks.

**How they work.** They tend the Bloom around Ashvale and teach from their sanctuary on the west edge of town. Their members carry copper-dark binding lines on the forearms.

**Who leads.** @[Master Speaker Tevan Lin].

**Who they argue with.** The Boundary Keepers, who guard the old restrictions.

## The Wrights

*Ashvale's makers: tools that work.*

**What they want.** Tools that work.

**How they work.** Ashvale's artisans make warding stones, listening bowls, walkers and grafted orchards. They pay well for salvage from the old waygate under the west road.

**Who leads.** @[Chief Wright Brenna Halvor].

**Who they argue with.** Anyone afraid of waking old work, which is most of Ashvale.

## The Bloom

*A weather of minds in the mycelium, grown through the ruins of Kaelith.*

The Bloom is not a single mind that speaks. It is a weather of minds in the mycelium, thinking by pulse and growth. It grew through the ruins of Kaelith after the Shrouding, and everything alive since then carries a little of it.

**Communion.** Communion opens a person to the Bloom, at a cost in self. Those who go too deep may stop knowing where they end.

**What the spore-bound can do.** Listen through roots. Read memories in old mycelia. Grow living houses. Heal with membranes that remember the body's intended form, though the membranes cannot take iron. Adaptive spores let a traveller pass through hostile Bloom for a while.

**What it learned.** The Bloom drank Kaelith's constructs and learned from them. Circuit-roots and Arbors followed.

## Glyph-Work and the First Grammar

*Kaelith's magic of call and answer: intention, resonance, pattern, world.*

Kaelith's glyph-work follows the First Grammar: intention wakes resonance, resonance makes a pattern, and the pattern becomes the world. It is the same grammar the Bloom speaks, in another voice.

Kaelith doors listen. Outer gates may demand a sequence of notes, a tuning fork, a key or marked blood.

**Glyph-scars.** Glyphs can be carved into living flesh, and some grow on their own. Carving them is forbidden in Kaelholm, and a rite in the Collective's Warband.

**Kaelith materials.** Everstone does not rot. Hatch-metal drives roots away.

**At the table.** Glyph-work uses ordinary arcane spells.

## The Myths of Aetheris

*What Aetheris tells about the beginning, the gods, the Cycle and the fall.*

| Age | What is told |
| --- | --- |
| Before the gods | There was Fire. The first flame still burns without fuel in @[The Ashen Wastes]. Some call it creation's source, others what remains at the end. |
| The whole world | One world existed, unbroken and eternal. |
| The Shattering | The Elders broke the Ethereal Crystal. |
| The age of gods | Gods fought at the Titan's Fall, and their colossal skeletons still lie there. Their weapons were made at the Nameless Forge. One god tried to sit the Shattered Throne, and failed. |
| The Cycle | Again and again a faction gathered the shards and remade the world. The Hidden Ones guided each turn, and erased whoever came too close to the truth. |
| Things erased | A war nobody remembers: the Bloodless War. A king who tried to seize the Loom: the Unraveled King. A city stolen from the surface and sunk: the Drowned City. Someone who tried to break the Aetherfall, and was unmade. |
| The Broken World | The last great kingdom fell. Its capital, Ashen Spire, is a ruin. The shards were lost again, and no faction holds power. |

## Money

*Copper, silver, gold and platinum, taken by weight on both continents.*

Both continents use copper, silver, gold and platinum coins. Each dominion and Kaelholm strikes its own faces on them, but coins are accepted by weight across every border. Western Kaelmar still relies heavily on barter.

**At the table.** Standard coin values: copper (cp), silver (sp), gold (gp) and platinum (pp).

## Languages

*Common, Kaelish, Tidecant, and the tongues of the factions.*

**Common and Kaelish.** Common is spoken in Aetheris and Kaelish in Kaelmar. They are distant cousins, close enough that patient speakers can be understood. Sailors on the Black Tides crossing mix the two into a trade pidgin called Tidecant.

| Tongue | Who speaks it |
| --- | --- |
| Veilspeech | The Shadow Pact's cant. |
| Root-tongue | The Wildborn's language. |
| The First Grammar | Kaelith's dead writing, read but never spoken. |
| Circle-tongue | The Spore Circle's language, used in communion. |

Every other common tongue of the peoples is spoken on both continents.

**At the table.** Common and Kaelish can count as one language. Veilspeech works as Thieves' Cant. Root-tongue is Druidic for druids and Sylvan for everyone else. The First Grammar is a rare language.

## Travel between continents

*Two weeks by sea in the dark, or through the Rift, if you dare.*

**By sea.** Ships cross the Black Tides between Kaelholm and the ports of the Eternal Veil in about two weeks. Crews douse every lantern over the trench where the Rift runs beneath the sunless sea. Regular crossings have only recently begun. Smugglers also use the route.

**Through the Rift.** The Rift has one open end in the Hollow Marches and another by Ashvale. Travellers can enter one end, cross the border of the Ethereal Realm, and come out of the other. Only seasoned and very brave travellers attempt it.
