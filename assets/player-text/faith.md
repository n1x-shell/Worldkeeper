# Faith in practice

## How to use this

How people worship the gods, on both continents: where, who, how, and which days are holy. Each section becomes that god's page in the handbook.

## The Gods

*Six living gods, three dead ones, and the same pantheon on both continents.*

Both continents pray to the same gods, and the gods answer: clerics and paladins get their spells from them, and nobody seriously doubts they're real. Ask a priest about the world before the Cycle broke, though, and you'll get silence. The gods don't remember it either.

Most people don't belong to one god. A soldier prays to Karrath before a fight, to Solenne when she's wounded, and to Sephane when her friend doesn't get up. Temples are built for one god, but shrines by the road usually have room for all six.

| God | What they keep | Who prays to them most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| @[Solenne, the Dawn Mother] | Light, law and mercy | Healers, judges, the Celestial Order |
| @[Karrath, the Red Victor] | War, strength and sworn oaths | Soldiers, duellists, the Crimson Legion |
| @[Ithiel, the Many-Eyed] | Knowledge, magic and the stars | Scholars, sailors, the Arcane Consortium |
| @[Ellun, the Maskwright] | Secrets, night and freedom | Thieves, runaways, the Shadow Pact |
| @[Orrowen, the Green Antler] | The wild, the seasons and beasts | Farmers, hunters, the Wildborn Clans |
| @[Sephane, Keeper of the Cairn] | Death, memory and names | Mourners, Hollow Refuge, anyone who carves a Name Stone |

Three gods are dead, and the places they died are still there: @[Maruun, the Sundered Tide], @[Aurex, the Throne-Breaker] and @[The Nameless Smith].

**What people say**

- The gods answer prayers. They just won't answer questions.
- Every roadside shrine has six niches. Some have a seventh, empty, and nobody says who it's for.

## Solenne, the Dawn Mother

*Light, law and mercy. The god of the Celestial Order, and of every healer who ever sat up with a fever.*

**Where she's worshipped.** Everywhere the sun rises, but loudest in the Radiant Ascendancy, where every hall in @[Empyrean Spire] is built without a corner for a shadow. Her hospitals stand in every dominion, even the Legion's, and nobody burns them. In Kaelmar her shrines are small and plain, kept by healers rather than knights.

**Who prays to her.** Healers, judges, magistrates, anyone sitting by a sickbed. The Celestial Order claims her outright. Her healers would rather you didn't confuse them.

**How.** Prayers at dawn, facing east, with open hands. A lamp is kept lit in her temples day and night; letting one go out is a bad omen. Oaths sworn before her are sworn with a hand on a lamp, and a liar is said to feel it burn.

**Her sign.** A sunrise: a half-circle with rays, usually in gold leaf or yellow paint.

**Holy days.** **First Light**, on the Spring Equinox: every household puts out its lamps the night before and relights them at dawn from a neighbour's. Feuds are supposed to end that morning. Some do.

**What people say**

- Her healers will treat anyone. Her knights won't.
- In the Purged Halls, every lamp still burns. Nobody refills them.

## Karrath, the Red Victor

*War, strength and sworn oaths. The god of the Crimson Legion, and of anyone who's made a promise with a blade in their hand.*

**Where he's worshipped.** In every barracks, arena and training yard. His great temple is the Great Arena in @[Varkhaal], where the sand is said to be consecrated by the blood of the champions. In Kaelmar, mercenary companies keep his altars, and the Ascendancy's soldiers swear to him.

**Who prays to him.** Soldiers, duellists, wrestlers, smiths who make blades, and anyone about to make an oath they mean to keep.

**How.** Before a fight, a soldier marks their forehead with a thumb of red. Oaths to Karrath are sworn on a weapon and can't be taken back. Breaking one is the only sin his priests care about. His priests don't preach; they referee.

**His sign.** A red hand, open, palm out. It means "I've sworn".

**Holy days.** **The Red Games**, at Midsummer: a tournament in Varkhaal's Great Arena that draws fighters from every dominion and both continents. Smaller games are held in every Legion town the same week.

**What people say**

- Karrath doesn't care who wins. He cares who kept their word.
- An oathbreaker's red hand is said to come off when they wash. That's how you tell.

## Ithiel, the Many-Eyed

*Knowledge, magic and the stars. The god of the Arcane Consortium, and of every sailor who ever steered by night.*

**Where she's worshipped.** In libraries, observatories and lighthouses. In @[Zepharyos] she has a temple in every tower, and the Nexus Spire's highest room is her observatory. In Kaelmar, the archive-monasteries of @[Kaelholm|SET-302] keep her as their patron; every scholar there is something like a priest.

**Who prays to her.** Scholars, mages, navigators, sailors, astrologers, and students the night before an examination.

**How.** By learning something and writing it down. Her prayers are questions, said aloud to the night sky. Her temples keep a book open on the altar, and anyone may add a line. Sailors leave a small polished mirror on her shrines, so she can see the way home.

**Her sign.** An eye inside a ring of stars.

**Holy days.** **The Long Watch**, on Dry Wind 20, when the summer sky is clearest: nobody sleeps, and everyone counts stars until dawn. Children get their first lesson in steering by the stars that night.

**What people say**

- Ithiel answers a question with a better question.
- On the Black Tides there are no stars to steer by. Sailors pray to her anyway.

## Ellun, the Maskwright

*Secrets, night and freedom. The god of the Shadow Pact, and of everyone who ever ran away.*

**Where they're worshipped.** Quietly, and everywhere. In @[Noctis] their temples are open to the street, and every worshipper wears a mask. Everywhere else, their shrines are hidden: a mask nailed under a bridge, a niche at the back of a tavern. In Kaelmar, Ellun is the god of the people who left: runaways, exiles and those who burned their own names.

**Who prays to them.** Thieves, spies, lovers, runaways, debtors, slaves who mean to be free, and anyone keeping a secret for a friend.

**How.** In whispers, after dark, with the face covered. A secret told to Ellun's shrine is safe; repeating one told to you is the worst thing a worshipper can do. Ellun's priests never give their names.

**Their sign.** A plain mask with no mouth.

**Holy days.** **Masking Night**, on Old Winds 30: everyone in the street wears a mask, and nothing done behind one counts in the morning. In Noctis it lasts a week.

**What people say**

- Ellun keeps every secret but one. Nobody knows which.
- A runaway who reaches an Ellun shrine can't be taken from it. Most hunters respect that. Some don't.

## Orrowen, the Green Antler

*The wild, the seasons and beasts. The god of the Wildborn Clans, and of every farmer who ever watched the sky for rain.*

**Where he's worshipped.** In groves, fields and at the edge of the woods. @[The Rootspire] is his greatest temple, and it's a tree. In Kaelmar he's prayed to in every village in the hills, and the Spore Circle honours him as the god of the Bloom's patience, which his priests in Aetheris find strange.

**Who prays to him.** Farmers, hunters, herders, foresters, midwives, and the Wildborn Clans.

**How.** Outdoors, never under a roof. The first of every harvest and the first blood of every hunt go back to the ground. Hunters ask the animal's pardon before the kill. His priests wear antlers of wood or bone.

**His sign.** A pair of antlers, often with leaves growing from them.

**Holy days.** **Seedwake**, on First Seed 1, when the first seed goes into the ground with a song. **Harvest Home**, on Harvest 30, when the last sheaf is left standing in the field for him.

**What people say**

- Orrowen doesn't punish. He just lets the winter in.
- The last sheaf is never cut. If it's gone in the morning, it'll be a good year.

## Sephane, Keeper of the Cairn

*Death, memory and names. The god of mourners and of @[Hollow Refuge], and the one people pray to most, in a world where things are forgotten.*

**Where she's worshipped.** At graves, cairns and the Name Stones that stand outside every village. In Hollow Refuge, the Lantern House is her house as much as Solenne's. In Kaelmar, Kaelholm's hall of rolls keeps every name in the city in her honour, and a name burned there is a name taken from her keeping.

**Who prays to her.** Mourners, gravediggers, midwives, record-keepers, old soldiers, and anyone afraid of being forgotten.

**How.** By saying names aloud. A Name Stone is a standing stone with names carved into it; anyone may add one, and nobody may scratch one out. Her priests are carvers. Prayers to her are lists: the names of the dead, said in order, so she knows who's coming.

**Her sign.** A cairn of three stones.

**Holy days.** **Naming Day**, on the Autumn Equinox: the names of everyone who died that year are read aloud and carved. In Hollow Refuge, the reading goes on all day, and every year someone hears a name nobody can place.

**What people say**

- If nobody says your name, Sephane loses you.
- Some Name Stones have names on them that nobody carved.

## Maruun, the Sundered Tide

*The sea, and the god who died of it.*

Maruun was the sea's god, and she's dead. She fell at @[The Titan's Fall], and the sea has been different since: crueller, emptier, and in the west, sunless. @[The Black Tides] are said to be where she bled.

**Who prays to her.** Nobody, officially. Sailors still mutter her name over the side, out of habit. @[The Drowned Saints] preach that she's only sleeping, and drown their converts to wake her.

**Holy days.** The Drowned Saints keep **the Sundering**, on Monsoon 1, when the rains come in off the sea. Sensible people stay away from the docks that day.

**What people say**

- A fisherman's prayer still ends with her name. Nobody remembers why.
- Her heart was a jewel, and someone has it.

## Aurex, the Throne-Breaker

*The god who tried to sit the Shattered Throne, and failed.*

Nobody knows what Aurex was god of. What everyone knows is how he died: he tried to sit @[The Shattered Throne], and the throne broke him instead. Nobody prays to him. Soldiers passing the Throne spit, for luck.

**What people say**

- Aurex wanted to rule the other gods. That's the story, anyway.
- If a god couldn't sit the Throne, nobody can. People keep trying.

## The Nameless Smith

*Maker of the gods' weapons. Its name was the first thing ever erased.*

The Smith forged the gods' weapons at @[The Nameless Forge], and then its name was taken, the first name ever lost. Smiths everywhere still honour it without knowing what to call it: the first blow on every new blade is struck in silence, for the Smith.

**What people say**

- A smith's mark has a blank space in it. That's for the Smith.
- The Forge still burns. Nobody feeds it.

## Calendar

*One 363-day year, shared by both continents, and its holy days.*

Both continents keep the same year of 363 days: twelve 30-day months in four seasons, plus three festival days that belong to no month. A week has seven days. It's 300 After Bloom (AB) in Kaelmar and Year 31 Unbound in Aetheris.

| Season | Months | Festivals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | Spring Dawn, First Seed, Monsoon | Spring Equinox, before Spring Dawn 1, is New Year's Day. |
| Summer | Midnight Sun, Dry Wind, Harvest | Midsummer falls on Midnight Sun 15. |
| Autumn | Fall, Last Seed, Old Winds | Autumn Equinox, before Fall 1. |
| Winter | Snowfall, Evernight, Winter's Dusk | Evernight Festival, between Evernight 30 and Winter's Dusk 1. |

**Holy days and festivals**

| When | Festival | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring Equinox | **First Light** (Solenne) | New Year's Day. Lamps are put out the night before and relit at dawn from a neighbour's. Feuds are supposed to end. |
| Spring Equinox, in Kaelmar | **Bloom Eve** | The night before, Kaelmar remembers the Shrouding. Families sit up with the doors shut, and open them together at dawn. |
| Spring Dawn 1, in Aetheris | **First Morning** | Nobody sleeps the night before. At dawn, people go outside to check the world is still there. It always has been, so far. |
| First Seed 1 | **Seedwake** (Orrowen) | The first seed is sung into the ground. |
| Monsoon 1 | **The Sundering** (Maruun) | The Drowned Saints mourn the sea's god at the docks. Everyone else stays home. |
| Midnight Sun 15 | **Midsummer** and **the Red Games** (Karrath) | Bonfires everywhere; the great tournament in Varkhaal's Great Arena. |
| Dry Wind 20 | **The Long Watch** (Ithiel) | A night of counting stars. Children learn to steer by them. |
| Harvest 30 | **Harvest Home** (Orrowen) | The last sheaf is left in the field. |
| Autumn Equinox | **Naming Day** (Sephane) | The year's dead are named and carved on the Name Stones. |
| Old Winds 30 | **Masking Night** (Ellun) | Masks in the street, and nothing done behind one counts. A week long in Noctis. |
| Evernight Festival | **Evernight** | The longest night. Every household keeps a light burning till dawn, and nobody travels. In Ashvale, the keeping song is sung at the stones all night. |

**What people say**

- Evernight is the one festival everybody keeps. Nobody wants to be the house with the dark window.
- In the Marches, both First Morning and Bloom Eve are kept now, since the ships started coming. Any excuse.
