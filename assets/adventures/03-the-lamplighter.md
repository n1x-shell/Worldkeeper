Title: The Lamplighter
Arc: The Loose Thread
Hook: HOOK-931
Levels: 1 to 10 (written for level 1)
Linked: LOC-506, ETH-501, NPC-371, NPC-374, NPC-372, NPC-373, NPC-375, FAC-503, CRE-502, CRE-501, NPC-376, HOOK-931

## Premise
Tonight the lamps of the Nameless Asylum [LOC-506] aren't lit, and nobody can say who usually lights them. A bed in the long ward is made up for someone nobody can name. A life in Inkling's book has gone blank but for one line: "He lights the lamps." Something is eating a resident out of everyone's memory, and by the time the last person forgets him, there will be nothing left of him to save.

The truth: the forgotten resident is the Lamplighter. He woke on the Verge's grey grass thirty-one years ago with a courier's satchel, no name and no past, and has lit the Asylum's lamps every night since. He is Lemmy Carrow [NPC-376], or the part of him that kept walking. He is the third end of the loose thread, and the only one that's alive. An old Name-Eater [CRE-502] followed the thread's pull into the Asylum, and he is its meal.

## Hook
Keeper Lantern [NPC-371] gathers everyone awake in the common hall. "Someone in this house is being forgotten. I can feel the count is wrong. I can't feel who is missing." Lantern asks the party, as residents, to find out who it is before the lamps should have been lit again tomorrow, "so that somebody remembers them."

If the party are travellers rather than residents, they arrive at the Asylum's gate in the dark, and Lantern asks the same of them, as guests.

## Stakes
- **A life.** If everyone forgets the Lamplighter before dawn-bell, he is Unmade: his Thinning goes to 6 in one step [SYS-907]. The third end of the thread is gone with him.
- **The Asylum.** Its rule is that nobody here dies forgotten. If it fails once, the residents stop believing it can keep them.
- **The party's own memories.** A Name-Eater that has learned the party's names is a Name-Eater with a taste for them.

## Opposition
- **The old Name-Eater**, a slick of grey that wears the faces of what it has eaten. It is wearing the Lamplighter's face, and walking the halls as him, while the man himself fades in a store-room he can't remember how to leave.
- **Its spawn**, small and eager, in the drains and under beds.
- **Time.** The Asylum's dawn-bell rings when the Verge's grey lightens, about eight hours away. Count it down in scenes, not minutes.

## Clues
- The Asylum's count is off by one. Lantern has counted the beds three times and got a different number each time.
- The made-up bed in the long ward has a courier's satchel under it, empty but for a cracked token stamped with a waystone.
- Inkling's [NPC-375] book has a life in it gone blank, except "He lights the lamps," and, in the margin, a crossed-out H, O and L.
- Sister Nobody [NPC-374] remembers tending someone's hands, burned at the fingertips, as if from lamp wicks. Not his face.
- Tessk [NPC-372] remembers bringing someone in, years ago, from the place on the grey grass where a road used to come through. She has a silver cord on her wrist. "Matched his," she says, then frowns. "Matched whose?"
- Brindle Quickfoot [NPC-373] owes someone three coppers, and can't think who. He has kept the coins on a nail on the Lost Shelf.
- The lamps themselves. Anyone who lights one feels, for a moment, someone else's hands doing it, and remembers a face, and loses it again.
- Grey slime in the drains, and wet prints that end at walls.
- The Lamplighter being seen in the halls, by someone who then forgets they saw him. The real Lamplighter can't leave the lamp-store.

## Obstacles
- Everyone, the party included, is forgetting. Once per scene, each character makes a Wisdom save, DC 10. On a failure, they forget one clue the party has found, unless it's written down (and the Name-Eater eats ink too: it smears the last line anyone wrote).
- The Asylum's residents are frightened and quarrel. Two hold that the forgotten one should be let go, "like the rest of us were."
- Greylings [CRE-501] come in from the Verge whenever a door is left open, whispering names.

## Encounters
Three fights, plus a hunt through the halls.
- **The drains.** Three Name-Eater spawn (oblex spawn) under the long ward. Each one killed returns one forgotten clue to the character nearest it.
- **The gate (when the party talks to Tessk).** The thread's pull has drawn the Verge's things to the Asylum. Three Greylings [CRE-501] come in on the grey wind, whispering names, and Tessk fights beside the party with a silver blade. A Greyling's touch makes a character forget a name they know until a long rest. Let the players choose which one, and make them play it.
- **The east wing (the hunt).** The Name-Eater, in the Lamplighter's face, leads the party on a chase. Two spawn drop from the ceiling of the dark east wing, where the lamps are out, while the old one slides away through the walls.
- **The lamp-store (climax).** The old Name-Eater, wearing the Lamplighter's face, and the man himself, slumped among the oil-jars. At level 1, the "old one" is an oblex spawn with maximum hit points that can't be surprised, plus two more spawn and a Greyling that came in with it. It fights to keep its meal, and each time it takes damage it drops a face it has worn, which shrieks a name the party has forgotten tonight (they remember it again).

## Rewards
- The **thread's third end**, the man himself. When the Name-Eater dies, the last memory it ate comes back to whoever is nearest: a name, Lemmy Carrow, and a road. A silver thread tied round his wrist now leads off into the grey toward the other two ends.
- The Asylum's thanks. Lantern writes each character's name in the Asylum's own book, which means somebody always remembers them. Once per long rest, a character in the book has Advantage on a Thinning save.
- Brindle's three coppers, which were always the Lamplighter's, and one item from the Lost Shelf for each character.
- Standing: +2 with the Veilborn [FAC-503].

## Approaches
- **Investigators** reconstruct the man from fragments, before the fragments are eaten too. The best tool is to write things down in more than one place.
- **Hunters** track the slime to the drains and work inward.
- **The kind** sit with the residents, keep them talking and help them remember each other. Each resident who is given back one of their own memories adds one clue.
- **The clever** ask what the Lamplighter does every night, and do it themselves: they light the lamps. The real Lamplighter, in the store-room, sees the light and calls out.

## Consequences
- If the party saves him, the Lamplighter is himself again, but only from the grey grass onward. Before that there's just a name and the feeling that he's late. He wants to deliver something, and can't remember what. (The party, if they've been to the Refuge or Ashvale, can tell him.)
- If they fail, the Lamplighter is Unmade before the dawn-bell. The Asylum keeps a bed made for him, which nobody can explain. Later, tying the thread back is impossible. Letting it go is the only ending left (see The Loose Thread [HOOK-931]).
- Sister Nobody tells the party, privately, that a Name-Eater this old doesn't wander. Something drew it here. The thread draws other things too.

## If ignored
The lamps aren't lit the next night either. Residents start losing each other, one name a night, starting with the newest arrivals. In a week the Asylum is a house of strangers who can't remember why they came, and Lantern sits at the gate counting beds.

## Scaling
- **Levels 3 and 4.** The old Name-Eater is an Echo [CRE-505] with the oblex's memory-eating, wearing the Lamplighter's face. Add a Threshold Hound [CRE-504] at the open door. Raise the forgetting save to DC 12.
- **Levels 5 to 10.** The Name-Eater is a true old one (Adult Oblex), and it has already eaten a resident the party knows: pick one of the five. A Lamp-moth Swarm [CRE-503] has put out every light in the east wing. Someone from outside is after the thread too: an agent of the Architects of Eternity [FAC-502], who bought a piece of the Fields from Brindle, has come to collect the man the thread is tied to. Raise the forgetting save to DC 14.

# Scene: The Count Is Wrong
Kind: scene
Linked: NPC-371, LOC-506

> The Nameless Asylum is quiet after dark, but not like this. Usually there's a small warm light at every door, and someone walking from one to the next with a taper. Tonight the halls are grey, lit only by the Verge's own dim not-quite-dusk through the high windows. In the common hall, Keeper Lantern stands with the lamp behind its chest-grille turned low, holding a list in iron fingers, and counting.

Lantern counts the residents aloud, gets a number, counts again, and gets another. It asks the party for help, plainly and without fuss. Then it gives them the list, which has a smudge on it where a name should be.

# Scene: The Long Ward
Kind: clue
Linked: NPC-375

The made-up bed, the satchel underneath it and the cracked waystone token. Inkling is sitting on the next bed with his book open, staring at a blank page. He can do any voice he's ever heard, and he tries, over and over, to do the voice of whoever this page was about. He gets as far as a dry, cheerful "Evening—" and loses it.

The drains under the long ward are where the spawn are.

# Scene: Fragments
Kind: social
Linked: NPC-374, NPC-372, NPC-373

Each resident holds one piece, and each piece costs something: a memory of the party's own given back, a promise, or a bit of honest company.

- **Sister Nobody**, in the infirmary: burned fingertips; and a cough "from the road. He'd say, from the road, like there was only one."
- **Tessk**, at the gate: the place on the grass where a road used to come through, and the cord on her wrist.
- **Brindle**, at the Lost Shelf: three coppers on a nail. He also sells the party, cheaply, a courier's cap he took off a peg in the lamp-store years ago. It has the same knot as the satchel.

The forgetting save happens once in this scene.

# Scene: At the Gate
Kind: encounter
Linked: NPC-372, CRE-501

> The Asylum's gate is an old church door in a wall of grey stone, set in the open grass with nothing on its far side but more grass. Tessk the Wayfinder sits on the step, sharpening a silver blade, and doesn't look up. "They're coming in," she says. "Your forgetting has a smell." Out on the grass, three low shapes are sliding toward the door, and you can hear them whispering. One of them is saying your name.

Fight the Greylings at the gate with Tessk. Afterward she'll talk.

# Scene: The Lamplighter in the Hall
Kind: encounter
Linked: CRE-502

Somewhere between fragments, a party member turns a corner and sees him: a lean man in a long coat with a taper, lighting nothing, who smiles and says, "Evening." Let them talk to him. He is pleasant and vague, and his shadow is wrong. If they follow him, he goes into a wall and is gone.

Insight (DC 12), or noticing that his taper has no flame, tells them it isn't a man. This is the Name-Eater wearing what it's eating.

If the party chases it, it leads them into the east wing, where the lamps are out, and two spawn drop from the dark ceiling while it slides away through the wall. Win, and the party finds the wet trail that leads to the lamp-store.

# Scene: Light the Lamps
Kind: clue

If the party lights even one lamp, every character who does so sees, for a heartbeat, someone else's burned hands doing it, and a face they almost know. If they light all of them (an hour's work), the Lamplighter, in the lamp-store, sees the light under the door and remembers the way out. He calls for help.

This is the fastest way to the climax, and the kindest.

# Scene: The Lamp-Store
Kind: climax
Linked: NPC-376, HOOK-931

> A low room lined with shelves of oil-jars and boxes of wicks, smelling of smoke and old wool. In the corner a man sits against the wall with his knees drawn up. He is greyer than he should be, so faint you can see the jars through him. Standing over him, with exactly his face and his coat, is something that isn't him, smiling.

Kill the Name-Eater, or drive it off with light (it hates the lamps once they're lit). When it dies, the last thing it ate comes back to the nearest person, who suddenly knows his name, says it aloud and, saying it, gives it back to him.

> "Carrow," he says, as if he's been looking for that word for thirty years. "Lemmy Carrow. Off the Crossroads." He looks at his wrist, where a silver thread is tied that wasn't there a moment ago, running out through the wall toward somewhere very far away. "I think I'm late."

He's the third end of the thread. He doesn't know what the other two are. He'd like to.
