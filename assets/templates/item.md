# Item

A magic or unusual object that could turn up in a pack, a shop or a hoard. Use an Artifact for a single object of great power and history.

## Facts

- **Rarity**: Give the rarity, from common to legendary. Example: Uncommon (Widow's Ring)
- **Attunement**: Say whether it needs attunement. Example: attunement (Widow's Ring)
- **Charges**: Give its charges and how they come back, if it has any. Example: 3 charges, regains 1d3 at dawn (Bell of Names)
- **Kind**: Say what kind of item it is in the rules. Example: wondrous item (Lamp-glyph That Won't Light for Liars)

## Description

> Guidance: What it looks like and how it feels to hold. A small sensory detail makes it memorable.
> Example (Bloom-bread, ITM-312): "A loaf that never goes stale and tastes faintly of rain."

## What it does

> Guidance: Its effect in plain words, as a character would describe it. Keep the story first and the numbers second.
> Example (Widow's Ring, ITM-001): "Holds one dead person's name. Nothing can make you forget them."

## Rules at the table

> Guidance: The exact effect in game terms: actions, ranges, saves and damage. Write it so a player can use it without asking.
> Example (Unstable Shard Fragment, ITM-012): "As a Magic action, throw it up to 60 feet: 6d6 force damage in a 15-foot radius, Dexterity save DC 15 for half."

## Limits and risks

> Guidance: What it cannot do, and what can go wrong. Limits stop an item from solving every problem.
> Example (Bell of Names, ITM-009): "It stays silent for anyone who has been erased."

## Secrets (GM only)

> Guidance: Anything about the item the players do not know, such as who made it or what else it does. Leave it empty if there is nothing to hide.
