# Vault Key

The key to one vault: not an object but a shape of willing minds acting together. Use a Vault for the place it opens, and an Item for a key that is a thing you carry.

## Facts

- **Opens**: Name the vault it opens. Example: Cinderlock (Cinderlock)
- **Shape**: Describe the shape of the key in a few words. Example: A fire, someone it burned, and someone who forgave it (Cinderlock)
- **What it asks**: Say what the key demands of the people who form it. Example: An enemy reconciled (Cinderlock)
- **Minds needed**: Give how many people must take part. Example: three (The Seed Vault)

## What it is

> Guidance: How this kind of key works, in a line or two. Players need to know it cannot simply be stolen.
> Example (The Seed Vault, VLT-301): "At a vault heart, the key is made of joined minds acting awake, willing and honestly."

## What it asks

> Guidance: What the people forming the key must do, be or give. This is the heart of the adventure to open it.
> Example (Hollowbell, VLT-313): "The keeping song, sung by thirteen people at once."

## Who could form it

> Guidance: The people in the world who fit the key, and why. Finding them is often the real quest.
> Example (Saltgate, VLT-309): "Its key requires a traveller from Kaelmar and a traveller from Aetheris."

## When it fails

> Guidance: What happens if someone lies, is forced or is not truly willing. Make failure clear before the party tries.
> Example (The Seed Vault, VLT-301): "A lie, charm or forced hand keeps the heart closed for one day."

## At the table

> Guidance: How to run the moment of opening, and what rolls do and do not do. Keep it about the people, not the dice.
> Example (The Seed Vault, VLT-301): "No ability check substitutes for the required participation."

## Secrets (GM only)

> Guidance: What the key really does to the people who form it, or what else it could open. Leave it empty if there is nothing to hide.
