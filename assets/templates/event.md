# Historical Event

Something that happened in the past and still shapes the present: a battle, founding, treaty, disaster or discovery. Use Cosmology for how the world itself works, and an Adventure Hook for something the party could take part in now.

## Facts

- **When**: Give the date in the calendar the players use, or say roughly when. Example: Year 2 Unbound, 271 AB (The Lantern Truce)
- **Where**: Name the place it happened. Example: Hollow Refuge (The Lantern Truce)
- **Who took part**: Name the people or groups involved. Example: the five factions (The Lantern Truce)

## What happened

> Guidance: The event as most people tell it, in a few plain lines. Tell it as a short story, not a list of dates.
> Example (The Lantern Truce, HIS-011): "Every envoy left a blade at the door, and every envoy walked out alive."

## Why it matters now

> Guidance: What changed because of it and what is still different today. This is the link between the history and the party's world.
> Example (The Lantern Truce, HIS-011): "Hollow Refuge has been neutral ground ever since, because every faction has needed it at least once"

## What is left of it

> Guidance: The places, objects, customs or scars it left behind that the party can still see. History is more useful when it can be visited.
> Example (The Raising of the Thirteen Stones, HIS-913): "No frost falls inside it, and it is a lock, not an altar."

## What people say

> Guidance: The versions, boasts and arguments people tell about it now. Different tellings are a gift to the GM.
> Example (The Lanternless Crossing, HIS-015): "Smugglers crossed for nearly twenty years before anyone ran a regular ship."

## Open questions

> Guidance: What nobody knows about it yet. Write these down so you can decide later, or let the party find out.
> Example (The Raising of the Thirteen Stones, HIS-913): "Who raised the stones, and when."

## Secrets (GM only)

> Guidance: What really happened, or the part of the story nobody tells. Note who knows the truth.
> Example (The Lantern Truce, HIS-011): "None of the five envoys remembers who sent the invitations."
