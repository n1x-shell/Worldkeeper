# Calling

The world's own name for a character class: what people call those who follow it, and how they are seen. Use a People or Species for where a character comes from, and a Faction for who they serve.

## Facts

- **Class**: Name the D&D 2024 class it is. Example: Fighter (Warrior)
- **Subclass names**: Name the subclasses that fit this calling. Example: Champion or Battle Master (Warrior)
- **Title**: Give the calling's title in the world. Example: The Iron Vanguard (Warrior)
- **Known in the world as**: Give the name ordinary people use. Example: Votary (Cleric)

## What the world calls it

> Guidance: The names people use for followers of this class, and which subclass goes by which name. Players will hear these names in play.
> Example (Wizard, CAL-019): "A Diviner is called a Mystic, a Bladesinger a Spellblade"

## Who they are

> Guidance: What followers of this calling do and what they are known for. Write it as a local would describe them.
> Example (Cleric, CAL-013): "Votaries serve a god directly: they heal in its name, speak for it and, when they have to, fight for it."

## In each land

> Guidance: How the calling looks in each continent or realm, one line each. This gives players a place to start their story.
> Example (Spellblade, CAL-007): "In Kaelmar, spellblades fight with glyph-cut blades and glyph-lances."

## How people see them

> Guidance: Where they are welcome, where they are feared and who hires them. This shapes how a character is greeted.
> Example (Spellblade, CAL-007): "The arenas of Varkhaal love them."

## At the table

> Guidance: The class and subclasses a player should pick, in one plain line. Point to the rules books in use.
> Example (Warrior, CAL-001): "A fighter, usually a Champion or a Battle Master."

## Notes for the GM (GM only)

> Guidance: Anything about this calling the players should not know yet, such as a hidden order or a cost. Leave it empty if there is nothing to hide.
