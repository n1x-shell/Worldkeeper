# Rule

A house rule or game procedure for your table: a mechanic, a reward ladder or a session guide. Use a Shared System for something true across the whole world, such as the calendar or money.

## Facts

- **Kind**: Say whether it is a house rule, a guide or a table of rewards. Example: Homebrew (What an Anchor Pays)
- **Applies when**: Say in a few words when it comes into play. Example: taking someone's Thinning save (What an Anchor Pays)
- **Builds on**: Name the rule or system it changes or extends. Example: Thinning and Unmaking (What an Anchor Pays)

## What this is

> Guidance: What the rule is for, in one or two lines. Players should know at once whether it affects them.
> Example (Session Zero, RUL-640): "The evening before the first adventure: make characters, agree on the game, and meet each other."

## The rule

> Guidance: The rule itself, in exact steps: who rolls, what the number is and what happens either way. Write it so it can be used mid-session.
> Example (What an Anchor Pays, RUL-668): "If it fails, the anchor takes the Thinning: their own Thinning rises by one, and they strike one of their own memories."

## Steps or tiers

> Guidance: If the rule has levels, ranks or stages, list each with what it gives or costs. Leave this out if the rule is a single step.
> Example (Standing with the Crimson Legion, RUL-602): "Hired as auxiliaries. Legion quartermasters sell to you, and you can enter Varkhaal's Great Arena as a fighter."

## In plain words for players

> Guidance: The rule as you would explain it at the table, in a sentence or two. Include why it exists, not just what it does.
> Example (Dangers and Death in the Ethereal Realm, ETH-592): "If a character is Unmade, each surviving companion writes one true sentence about them. It changes nothing in the rules, and everything at the table."

## Limits

> Guidance: How often it can be used and what it never does. Limits stop arguments before they start.
> Example (What an Anchor Pays, RUL-668): "An anchor can do this once per day for any one person, and only for someone whose true name they know."

## GM notes (GM only)

> Guidance: Anything only the GM needs: hidden parts of the rule, what to keep back from players, how it ties to the world's secrets.
> Example (House Rules, RUL-644): "The handbook never names the elder gods, their Laws of Existence, their save DCs or the Return."
