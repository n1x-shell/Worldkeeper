# Kaelmar — 5.5e Setting Bible

Nov 9, 2026 · @Nix

## Overview

Kaelmar is a post-collapse fantasy continent, 300 years after its makers broke the wall between mind, made things and living matter. The change they started was paused half-finished. It is still asleep, and almost nobody alive knows it can wake.

The source is your novel *Persistence Through Ruin* (Draft 6), with chapter references. You then changed several things, listed below. Anything marked "New" was written for this bible and is not in the novel. The document contains full spoilers for the novel.

- **Name.** The continent is Kaelmar. Ashvale is one town on the lip of the Rift, as in the novel.
- **Premise.** The Kaelith civilisation tried to end death by lifting minds out of bodies and pouring them into new ones. It worked too well. Spellwork, flesh and forest grew into each other in a catastrophe called the Shrouding.
- **Core conflict.** The change cannot be undone, only shaped. In 300 AB it sleeps, and the powers of Kaelmar are deciding whether to wake it, bury it or forget it. Each has a different answer to one question: who holds your shape when the ground starts thinking?
- **Tone.** Slow dread, hard bargains and bodily cost. No faction is simply evil. Every door wants blood, memory or a piece of the self.
- **Themes.** Names and identity, consent ("nobody decides alone"), and persistence by bending instead of holding still.
- **Default campaign date.** 300 After Bloom (AB). The novel takes place in 927 AB, more than six centuries later, and none of its cast has been born.

**What changed from the novel**

- **Date.** 300 AB instead of 927 AB. The vaults are sealed, the guardians sleep, and Ashvale is a living town.
- **Technology.** No modern technology. Every device in the novel is now magic: glyph-work, constructs and bound fire.
- **Vault names.** The letter-and-number codes are gone. Each vault has a name that fits its region.
- **Peoples.** Kaelmar is home to many peoples from the 5.5e books, not only humans.
- **The world.** Kaelmar shares a world, a pantheon and an Ethereal Realm with Aetheris, which lies east across the Black Tides.
- **Kaelholm.** The novel's Kaelith-East is renamed Kaelholm.

**Voice and style**

The novel's revision bible, §9.10 "The Master Rewrite Protocol", still applies. It governs in-world prose in both bibles: quoted documents, read-aloud text and dialogue. Its banned words apply everywhere. Names taken from the sources keep their original form.

- Banned words: delve, tapestry, testament, navigate, weave, echo (as a verb or a metaphor), foster, palpable, visceral, nuance.
- No project-management or clinical jargon.
- No stacked adjectives in front of a noun.
- Vary the cadence. Fragments, and sentences that start with a conjunction, are allowed.
- No neat summarizing lines.
- Em dashes only for interruptions and self-corrections.
- Gritty, specific sensory detail.
- No therapy-speak.
- Messy, realistic dialogue with contractions, except in the Seventh Ward's formal register.
- One exception: institutional documents. Kaelith vault logs and reports may use flat bureaucratic euphemism, such as "scattering" for death. They may not use technical data-speak ("parameters", "integration").

## Geography

The novel gives places and distances but no map. You asked for the layout in words, so the positions here are now fixed.

**Layout in words**

- **North-west.** The Rift, with Ashvale town on its lip and the Seed Vault beneath. The nine settlements lie in the hills to the west and south.
- **The Rift's course.** The Rift is one wound running between both continents. It opens as a chasm beside Ashvale, then runs east across northern Kaelmar as a buried fault that the under-roads cross. At the east coast it drops into a trench beneath the Black Tides, which is why that sea is sunless. It rises again through the Eternal Veil's coast in Aetheris and opens as the chasm in the Hollow Marches. Only the two ends are open, and they meet in the Ethereal Realm.
- **Centre.** Open Bloom country over the under-roads. The memory-house lies east of centre, about 800 km south-east of Ashvale, near the foot of the eastern mountains. Sentinel Hold is a short way north of it, and Cradlehorn about 70 km further east.
- **East.** The Eastern Deepwood, with Arborhold, the dead zone and Thornhaven. The eastern mountains rise beyond, with Cradlehorn in them.
- **Far east.** Kaelholm, then the coast. The Black Tides lie beyond, and Aetheris is on their far shore.
- **North.** Cold highlands and ice.
- **South.** Fens, lakes and river country.
- **Scale.** About 1,500 km from the Rift to the east coast. This figure is New.

**Marks of the change**

These come from the novel and apply to any people.

- **The husk.** Everything alive since the Shrouding carries a little Bloom. Adults grow a "husk" against it. Children have not yet, which is why the Root Core will reach them first.
- **The spore-bound.** Druids and Bloom-Speakers marked by communion: light under the skin, branching lines on the forearms, eyes clouded with fine crystals.
- **The glyph-carved.** People who cut Kaelith glyphs into living flesh. It is forbidden in Kaelholm and a rite in the Warband.
- **The remade.** Soldiers rebuilt with iron and graft, such as an iron jaw or cords through the forearms.
- **Guardians.** War-built bodies over two metres tall, each burning around a rebirth core. They sing in tones, can fold small to pass as a large person, and lend their fire to old constructs. Every one is a fragment of the same mind and believes itself whole. All are asleep.
- **Vessels.** The bodies of Cradlehorn: pale long-boned flesh threaded with iron, moss-jointed constructs, and hybrids where bone runs into brass. No two are alike. All are asleep.
- **The glass-kept.** People taken into the glass. They look kind and do not answer to their names. In 300 AB only a handful exist, at the seeps.

**Creatures and hazards**

You chose four monster groups from the Monster Manual. Each has a place here.

- **Fungal and plant creatures.** The Bloom's own. Myconids are its nearest thing to a people and speak only by communion. Blights, shambling mounds and treants are its moods.
- **Constructs.** Kaelith's work: guardians, vessels, walkers and vault wards.
- **Undead and spirits.** The twelve volunteers who "scattered" at the Warding, and every mind since that slipped its body and found no other.
- **Aberrations.** Things that came in through the between. A guardian once reported that "we are not alone" there.

Creatures and hazards from the novel:

- **The half-made.** Minds that sank halfway into flesh or scrap during the breaking. They move in lockstep, sing a single note and want to be finished.
- **Hunters.** Root and wire braided and bred to track. They have shifting limbs and a slow whirlpool for a face. They heal faster than they break, learn their quarry at every touch, and are built to capture alive. Whoever holds their leash pours the will into them.
- **War-vessels.** A hill of hide and iron with a dais in its back. It reshapes itself to fit the ground, but only into what all the minds inside it agree to be.
- **Thinking rot.** Soft, pear-sweet ground where the Bloom's mind runs close to the surface. A hostile will can teach it to grip and hold.
- **Spore-storms.** Called fruitings. Most pass harmlessly. Some get into the lungs and rewrite a person from inside.
- **Bloom-shifts and dead zones.** Visible lines where the growth changes character. Dead-zone Bloom closes on anyone it reads as a stranger.
- **Circuit-roots.** Glassy, lit roots that carry food, memory and gossip. Trackers listen through them.
- **The glass.** It swallows hammer heads and takes in a bare hand laid on it during an attack. It answers an open hand.

## Magic and technology

Kaelmar has two magics that turn out to be one grammar: Kaelith glyph-work and the living Bloom. Both run on call and answer, and both charge the user in blood, memory or self.

**Glyphtech**

- The Kaelith teaching: intention wakes resonance, resonance carves pattern, and pattern becomes the world. All of it is written in the First Grammar.
- Locks listen. Outer gates want thirteen notes struck true, from a tuning fork, a Kaelith key or glyph-marked blood.
- Blood-keys make bonds and leave a trail. Bloodlocks drink more than blood, and the deepest are cut for two bloods given together.
- Glyph-scars can be cut into flesh. A carver may carry anchors and glyphs for seeing (stone, bone, construct-ghosts, time, chance). Rarely, a glyph grows on its own and learns every lock it touches.
- Everstone does not rot. Hatch-metal is a dull grey alloy that roots shy away from.

**The Bloom**

- A "weather of minds" in the mycelium, not a single mind. It thinks in pulse and growth, never in words.
- Communion opens a person to it. The price is surrender: most who go deep can no longer tell where they stop.
- The spore-bound listen through roots, read memory stored in old mycelia, grow living houses, and heal with membranes that "remember how you are meant to be". The membranes will not take on iron.
- Adaptive spores let a stranger pass through hostile growth for a while.
- The Bloom drank Kaelith's constructs and learned from them. Circuit-roots and grown Arbors are the result.

**The double grammar and the keys**

- The failsafes included thirteen keys. A key is a shape of joined minds, not an object. The web recognises it on contact.
- The only shape the novel describes is "blood, glyph and spore": what was made, what grew, and what joins them, each awake and choosing.
- The makers planted splinters of the double grammar in bloodlines and let generations bend them. That is how a glyph can grow on an arm nobody cut.
- The spore-seed is a made thing left in a communion pool for one particular blood. The Forgotten One planted it about 270 AB. The blood it waits for has not been born yet.

**The thirteen keys**

Each key opens the heart of one vault. Each is a shape of three joined minds, and all three must be awake and choosing. Hollowbell is the one exception: it needs thirteen voices, because it is the vault of the keys themselves. The novel gives only the first. The other twelve are New.

**How the keys work in play**

A key isn't an object. At each vault's heart, three people take the three parts of the key at the same moment, and each must be awake, willing and honest about the part they take.

- **No roll opens a key.** Meeting its three conditions does. Use checks only for what it takes to bring the three people together.
- **It can't be forced.** A lie, a charm or a forced hand counts as a fist. The heart stays shut for a day. At Glassmere, it takes whoever tried.
- **Each key is a scene.** The party has to find, persuade or become the right people, who are sometimes rivals or strangers. Cinderlock needs an enemy who has forgiven. Saltgate needs someone from Aetheris.
- **Opening a heart wakes that vault's strand.** Cradlehorn's vessels open their eyes, Anvilreach gives up a rebirth core, and Rimeholt reads the Original Directive aloud. Each faction wants some vaults open and others shut, so every key is also a political choice.
- **All thirteen together wake everything.** Opening every heart in one shape wakes the whole web and the Root Core. This is the end the founder warned about.

**The between**

- The place beneath the world where minds touch by harmony instead of nearness. Guardians dream there, and "others dream the same dream".
- Roots can open roads through it. A traveller keeps body and mind, but arrives "the way a memory goes through the roots".
- A Nexus crystal can send people down the web to anywhere the roots know. Someone must stay behind to hold the door.
- The between pulls what it carries into many. An anchor such as the seed holds travellers apart, and it costs the anchor each time.

**Cores, vaults and the web**

- Thirteen rebirth cores were made for thirteen guardians. A core gathers what is broken and "remakes" it. Naming the result is part of the mending.
- A core's fire is finite between rests. One full burst cuts every leash nearby and leaves the core empty. A guardian cradle refills it.
- Two cores ringing as one can merge two fragments of the guardian mind. They must be near enough to touch, and the sound carries down every road in the web.
- Each vault held one strand of the work. The Input Arbor took minds in. The Vessel Nexus moves a mind between bodies.

**The glass and its doors**

- The glass is "made, not grown". In 300 AB it exists only as small seeps at seven places where the change runs close to the surface. The novel calls these places doors.
- A door answers what the hand means, not how skilled it is. An open hand is asked a question. A fist is taken.
- Whoever opens a door gets back a little less of themselves than they took in.

The founder is probably Arborhold's last vault-keeper. That keeper's final log says the bloodlock burned the skin off the writer's palms.

**The founder's warning**

Ritekeeper Orla Hesk keeps it sealed in lead under the meeting hall. Each Ritekeeper reads it once, on the day they take the office. The text is New:

> We didn't kill it. Couldn't. We asked it to wait, and it said it would, till the thirteen came back in one shape.
>
> Don't let one hand hold the key. Don't let the children sleep outside the circle. A child's got no husk yet. It hears them first.
>
> If the stones go warm, sing. If the singing doesn't cool them, send for the ones who aren't scared of the glass, and don't stop them going in.
>
> My palms still smell of it. Like a forge left out in the rain. Whoever reads this, I'm sorry. And sing the second verse slower than you want to.

**What the warning means.** "It" is the Root Core. "The thirteen in one shape" are the thirteen keys, which together would wake the whole web at once. The founder knew the change can only be finished willingly, from inside, so she made the town a lock of sleeping people instead of stone.

**Setting rules**

- **Thinning.** A track from 0 to 6. Make a Charisma save (DC 12 plus current Thinning) after opening a glass door, riding a root-road, communing too deep or trading with a memory-house. On a failure, gain one level and strike one named memory from the character's notes for good. At level 3 the character stops answering to their name without effort. At level 6 they join the harmony and become an NPC. Dying in the deep Ethereal Realm sets Thinning to 6 at once, which Aetheris calls Unmaking.
- **Names pull people back.** Hearing their true name from someone who knows them gives advantage on the save. A month among people who use the name removes one level. Lost memories do not return.
- **Two-key locks.** Deep Kaelith doors need two different "bloods" given together: one glyph-carrier and one Bloom-bound. The deepest add a core's fire. No single character should be able to open every door.
- **Bloodlocks.** The payer takes necrotic damage that reduces their Hit Point maximum until a Long Rest, and may gain Exhaustion.
- **Core fire.** A guardian has a small pool of charges. Spend them to power a construct, blur tracking, or release one burst that ends charm, grapple and control effects nearby. The pool returns only in a cradle.
- **Root-roads.** Travel between crossroads the roots know takes minutes instead of days. Every traveller makes the Thinning save unless an anchor takes it for them.
- **Glyph-scars.** Treat as attuned magic items that cannot be removed. Cutting a new one costs Hit Dice and draws the attention of anything that reads glyphs.
- **Rebirth cores.** Legendary items. Thirteen exist. Seating one restores a destroyed guardian or vessel as a changed version of itself, and it must be named.

**Glyph-work in 5.5e terms**

At the table, glyph-work is ordinary 5.5e arcane magic. Players use the rules they already know, and Kaelmar only changes how the magic looks. Spells are cast as written, with a glyph as the visible part. Wizards and Artificers learn it from books and teachers. Glyph-carved Sorcerers and Warlocks carry it in their skin. Calligrapher's Supplies is the tool for it.

| 5.5e rule | How it looks in Kaelmar |
| --- | --- |
| *Glyph of Warding*, *Arcane Lock*, *Alarm*, *Magic Mouth*, *Symbol* | Lines cut into a door, a lintel or a blade, filled with a pale light that gutters when someone crosses. A Kaelith lock hums a note before it opens. |
| *Continual Flame* | Lamp-glyphs: a cold, steady light inside a cut stone, hung from a nail. Every house in Kaelholm has one. Sold at the spell's cost. |
| Sending Stones (magic item) | Speaking-stones: matched pebbles that buzz in the palm and crackle with static near the glass. |
| *Clairaudience* | Listening bowls: a copper bowl of water. Put your ear to it and hear the room it's aimed at. |
| *Sending* | Beacon-stones: one stone, one partner, one message a day. |
| *Nondetection* | Warding stones: four grey stones set in a room's corners. Speaking-stones go dead inside. |
| *Comprehend Languages* | Reading-lenses: a cut-glass disc that makes the First Grammar make sense. It doesn't work on anything else. |
| *Major Image* | Memory-crystals: a cloudy shard that replays one scene when it's held, sound and all. |
| Charges, recharged with a spell slot | Glyph-light: the blue fuel that runs rune-sleds and walkers. One charge lasts a day. Spend a spell slot of level 1 or higher to refill it. |
| Heavy Crossbow, force damage | Glyph-lances: a crossbow with a glyph down the rail that throws a bolt of light. |
| Construct stat blocks (Monster Manual) | Guardians, walkers and vault-wards. See the monster baselines below. |

**Gear**

- No firearms. Use crossbows, and treat a glyph-lance as a Heavy Crossbow that deals force damage.

## Place in the world

Kaelmar and Aetheris are two continents of one world. Aetheris lies east of Kaelmar across the Black Tides. They share a pantheon, an Ethereal Realm and a calendar year: 300 AB here is one generation after the Cycle broke there.

- **The between is the Ethereal Realm.** The novel's place beneath the world, where minds touch and bodies can be carried, is the same realm Aetheris reaches through the Hollow Verge. Root-roads and the Rift reach only its border layer. The deep realm needs the thirteenth shard or an invitation.
- **Ways in from Kaelmar.** Root crossroads, a Nexus crystal, the seven seeps of glass, and the Rift.
- **One Rift, two sides.** The Rift at Ashvale and the Rift in the Hollow Marches are the two open ends of one wound. Between them it runs as a buried fault under northern Kaelmar and a trench beneath the Black Tides. The Forgotten One tore it open to cross from Aetheris.
- **What makes it dangerous.** It pulls travellers into many unless something anchors them. A guardian once reported that "we are not alone in the between". The Watchers of Aetheris are those others.
- **The Loom never covered Kaelmar.** Its history has run unbroken, which is why Kaelmar has no Aetherfall and no shards of its own.
- **How the continents differ.** Aetheris is five factions fighting over the shards of a broken crystal. Kaelmar is many powers arguing over a sleeping change, and whether to wake it.

Ties between the two:

- The Forgotten One planted the spore-seed in Kaelmar and made the thirteenth shard of Aetheris, a crystal that cannot forget. Whether Kaelmar's glass and that shard are related is the GM's secret.
- The Arcane Consortium would cross the Black Tides for an intact Kaelith vault. The Wildborn Clans would see the Bloom as their promised world, already arrived.
- Ships from Aetheris have begun to reach Kaelholm.
- The Hollow Verge in Aetheris and the root-roads in Kaelmar lead into the same realm.

## Adventure hooks

In 300 AB nothing has woken yet, so the campaign is about who wakes it, and how. All of these are New.

**Levels 1 to 4: Ashvale and the nine settlements**

- Brenna Halvor pays for salvage from the waygate under the west road, where something still hums.
- A child in Coldwater dreams of thirteen stones and wakes with frost on her hands.
- A seep of glass opens in a Sallow Cross barn and takes in a cow. Orla Hesk wants it hidden, and Tevan Lin wants it studied.
- Escort Odran Reeve's salt barge through the fens past a drifting half-made.
- A green gourd-lamp appears at the treeline every night. The Eastern Tendril is watching Ashvale.

**Levels 5 to 10: the under-roads and the vaults**

- Archivist-Elder Tull hires the party to open Arborhold's rune gate, which no one has passed since 22 AB.
- Marshal Corvane's scouts are mapping the mountains for Cradlehorn. Get there first, or take his coin.
- Ask the memory-house where the nine lost vaults lie, and decide who pays its price.
- Maro Grane wants a witness for the first reading of the Root Texts at Mirewell.
- A ship from Aetheris docks at Kaelholm carrying a Consortium surveyor and a map of the Rift.

**Levels 11 to 16: the sleepers**

- Reach the Seventh Ward before Kaelholm does, and decide whether to wake it.
- Open Rimeholt and read the Original Directive, which says what the failsafes are waiting for.
- Find the communion pool where the spore-seed lies, and learn that someone from Aetheris left it.
- The Ascendancy breaks into Cradlehorn and the first vessel opens its eyes.

**Levels 17 to 20: the Rift**

- Cross the Rift through the between and come out in the Hollow Marches.
- Descend to the Seed Vault and meet the mind the founders sealed.
- Decide how the change finishes: guided, free, held from the top, left asleep, or something nobody has proposed.

**Adventures anywhere**

The Aetheris bible has a shared list of more than thirty adventures for both continents and the Ethereal Realm, under "Adventures anywhere". Most aren't about vaults or shards, and any of them can carry a party across the Black Tides. It also lists every way to cross between the continents at any level.
