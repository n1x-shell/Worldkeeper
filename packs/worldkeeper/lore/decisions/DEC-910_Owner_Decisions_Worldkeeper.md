# Owner Decisions (Worldkeeper)

## How to read this
Decisions the owner has made since the archive was reconciled. Where one changes something the archive says, the archive's text is left as it was and this register says which reading is current. See also Resolved by Owner [DEC-902] and the Canon Conflict Register [DEC-903].

## The world is called Oros (November 2026)
**Current reading.** The world of Aetheris and Kaelmar is called Oros. It is the name its own peoples give their world.

**What it changes.** The archive calls it "the world" or "The World" throughout, and those lines stay as written: read "Oros" where they mean the world itself. The Ethereal Realm lies outside Oros, as before.

**What nobody has decided.** Where the name comes from, which tongue it is from, and whether Aetheris and Kaelmar both use it in the same form: UNDEFINED.

## The Loom of Eternity shapes both continents (November 2026)
**Current reading.** The Loom of Eternity [ETH-505] is what governs the fates of the people of Aetheris and Kaelmar alike. It shapes the peoples and the world around them. It can be tweaked, though by whom and how is a mystery. It is protected and guarded.

**What it changes.** The Loom as a force [COS-905] and the Loom [ETH-505] say Kaelmar was never covered by the Loom. That line is superseded: Kaelmar is on the Loom too.

**What stays.** The Forgotten One [NPC-901] cut the shards from the Loom about 270 AB, and that broke the Cycle: the shards can no longer tell the Loom to remake the world. The Loom itself still weaves.

**What nobody knows.** Why the Hidden Ones [FAC-007] and the elder gods made the world of Aetheris and Kaelmar, and what the Loom is for. Breaking the Cycle didn't answer either question. The people and the world are still shaped, or at least influenced, by unseen forces.

## The Ethereal Realm lies outside the world (November 2026)
The Ethereal Realm [CON-501] lies outside the world. Whoever holds power there could restart the Cycle, create new worlds, or destroy reality itself. It holds cosmic powers: the Watchers [COS-904], the Hidden Ones, the elder gods and other entities, each with its own conflicts and goals. They mostly overlook people coming and going, unless tested. Now that the Cycle is broken, reaching the realm is possible. See The Depths of the Ethereal Realm [COS-021].

## The Ethereal Orb and the endings (November 2026)
The Orb is back. The World Canon Rules [WLD-902] removed the books' Orb; that line is superseded. The Ethereal Orb is the Ethereal Crystal made whole. Since the Cycle broke, it can only be formed in the Ethereal Realm. Twelve shards form the Ethereal Orb: its bearer ascends and remakes the world until they fall, and then the Cycle restarts. Thirteen form the Greater Ethereal Orb: it heals both continents, closes the Rift and the realm, and restores every name and memory, and everyone who came is shut in the realm for life. Full details in The Ethereal Orb [COS-022]. Eleven of the twelve with the thirteenth form the Lesser Ethereal Orb: a mad god, an unfinished Aetherfall, the realm spilling into the world and mass forgetting, flavoured by whichever shard is missing, until the god falls. In the world, the legends about the thirteenth disagree on purpose.

## The elder gods (November 2026)
Seven elder gods made the world, older than the Hidden Ones and the gods. They made it as a hiding place from the Watchers [COS-904], and what they hid is the people: the last seed of a kind of life the Watchers ended everywhere else. The Loom is its lock. The Hidden Ones are their stewards, and the Cycle was the stewards' idea. Today's gods are their children and don't know it.

The seven split over the first civilisation [COS-901]. Three taught it to cheat death, build doors out of the world and call beyond it, which drew the Watchers. Those three left past the Black Horizon. The other four sleep, one under each of the four shallow depths of the Ethereal Realm. The three are coming back, bringing Guests they bargained with out there: vast, incomprehensible things that think the world has been promised to them. Their return is a clock that advances in play, and it jumps to the end if anyone forms an Orb. The Nameless Sepulcher holds the first civilisation's last ruler, the teachers' pupil, who wants to take the elder gods' place. The Black Sun's eclipse is the Return; High Prophet Azhir knows, and is slowing his own cult. Mortals know only fragments. The elder gods have alien names. They and the Guests have Laws of Existence instead of statistics: invincible, not unstoppable. Their manifestations and heralds can be fought; the gods can be bound by their true names, cut off, put back to sleep or turned back. See The Elder Gods [COS-030] and Elder Gods at the Table [RUL-621].

## The Bloom-shaped species belong to Kaelmar (November 2026)
The Bloom happened in Kaelmar, so the species its spore-storms rewrote are Kaelmar's: the Bloomborn [PPL-010], Centaurs [PPL-015], Harengon [PPL-033], Kenku [PPL-035], Satyrs [PPL-037], Shifters [PPL-039] and Tabaxi [PPL-040]. Each carries the mark "Shaped by the Bloom" (tag origin/bloom). Those also found in Aetheris still appear under Aetheris. The Aarakocra [PPL-011] are left as they were: whether the Bloom shaped them is still disputed.

## Sixteen callings, one for every class (November 2026)
The archive's ten callings stand as written. Six more join them so that every class has a calling: Reaver (barbarian) [CAL-011], Loresinger (bard) [CAL-012], Votary (cleric) [CAL-013], Wildsoul (sorcerer) [CAL-014], Pactbound (warlock) [CAL-015] and Wright (artificer) [CAL-016]. Each calling's page lists the subclasses that fit it, from the Player's Handbook (2024), Xanathar's Guide to Everything, Tasha's Cauldron of Everything and Eberron: Forge of the Artificer. Spelljammer and Monsters of the Multiverse add no classes. Subclasses written for the 2014 rules are marked by book and used with the GM's permission; where the 2024 Player's Handbook reprints one, its version is the one used.

## The Spelljammer species live in the Ethereal Realm (November 2026)
The six species of Spelljammer: Adventures in Space belong to the Ethereal Realm, with their book origins carried into the world: the Astral Plane's silver void is the silver void of the Fields of Lost Realities, and lost homeworlds are worlds the Cycle threw away. Astral Elves [PPL-047] left Aetheris during an Aetherfall to be nearer their gods; Autognomes [PPL-048] are made by Kaelholm's glyph-wright gnomes and sent into the realm; the Giff [PPL-049] come from a discarded world whose forgotten gods drift petrified in the void; the Hadozee [PPL-050] come from Yazir, a forest world adrift in the Fields; Plasmoids [PPL-051] are native to the void and nobody knows how; Thri-kreen [PPL-052] appear on every desert world in the Fields. The Air Genasi are left for later.

## The Ethereal Realm's people and its new species (November 2026)
The realm's people now include its new species. Vantis Orm [NPC-397] is an astral elf and Brakka Twice-Born [NPC-395] a giff; their secrets are unchanged. Six people join them, one of each species: Lady Sennith Velarys [NPC-399] (astral elf), Gauge [NPC-400] (autognome), Major Halloway Stroud [NPC-401] (giff), Captain Chiri Akko [NPC-402] (hadozee), Ooma [NPC-403] (plasmoid) and Tch'kala [NPC-404] (thri-kreen). The archive's own people of the realm keep the species the archive gives them. On the pages of the people added by Worldkeeper, the Calling column names a calling (Mage, Wright, Loresinger and so on) rather than a class.

## The November audit (November 2026)
An audit of people, hooks and loot made these changes. Three people added by Worldkeeper are renamed so that no two people share a name they shouldn't: Little Quell is now Little Hush [NPC-398], Old Pell Ambry is now Old Corwin Ambry [NPC-377], and Scout-Captain Hesk Varn is now Scout-Captain Teague Dunmarsh [NPC-338]. A surname is reused only where the lore means it to be (a family, or Sel Marrow's unexplained tie to the Marrows). Every person's handbook page now gives a class and the name the world knows them by (see Classes first, below); the archive's own records are unchanged. In the treasure pools, items now sit at their rulebook rarity: All-Purpose Tool, +1 is uncommon, Well of Many Worlds is legendary, the Staff of the Woodlands is rare, and the Moon Sickle that has no common version is replaced by Boots of False Tracks.

## Classes first, callings as the world's names (November 2026)
This supersedes the sixteen callings. The handbook is organised by the thirteen classes of the books in use, because players and the GM think in classes. A calling is what the world calls someone, not a class: a fighter is a Warrior, a wizard a Mage, a rogue a Silent Blade. Each class has a page listing every subclass and the names the world uses. The six callings added earlier now name classes: Reaver is the Barbarian [CAL-011], Loresinger the Bard [CAL-012], Votary the Cleric [CAL-013], Wildsoul the Sorcerer [CAL-014], Pactbound the Warlock [CAL-015] and Wright the Artificer [CAL-016]. Druid [CAL-017], Fighter [CAL-018] and Wizard [CAL-019] are added. The archive's ten callings stand as written: Rogue, Ranger, Paladin and Monk are also class pages, and Warrior, Mage, Shaman, Necromancer, Spellblade and Mystic are kept as the world's names. On every person's handbook page, the table gives their Class and what they're Known as; the class comes from the GM's NPC builds, except where it would give away a secret (Harbourmaster Gulen Saltback [NPC-369] and Old Ash [NPC-361] show Unknown).


## Residents of the landmarks, vaults and buildings (November 2026)
Every landmark, vault and building now has someone there: 62 new people (NPC-405 to NPC-466, in packs/worldkeeper/lore/residents), one or two to a place, each with a public face and, where it fits, a GM-only secret. ## The Stormborn are air genasi (November 2026)
The Stormborn [PPL-013] are this world's air genasi, and use the air genasi rules from Monsters of the Multiverse. The goliath option is dropped, and with it the storm giant in the family: some Stormborn are tall and broad, others slight, but all are air genasi at the table. There is no separate Air Genasi people. Without the book, use the stormborn rules (the world's own, open to all).

## The Return's omens and the faction web (November 2026)
The Return [COS-032] is tracked in the app as a seven-stage clock that only the GM sees; forming any Orb jumps it to stage 6. Players never see the clock: each stage it reaches shows them an omen and two rumours (packs/worldkeeper/return/omens.json), which never name the elder gods. The faction web draws its ties from the library's own links between factions and cults. A tie is public when a faction's handbook page names the other in its "Who opposes them" line; every other tie, alliances and cult ties included, players see only once the GM reveals it, and nothing about a faction they can't read about is ever shown.

## The session zero kit (November 2026)
The handbook has a session zero kit: Session Zero [RUL-640], a one-page primer for each start (Hollow Refuge [RUL-641], Ashvale [RUL-642] and the Nameless Asylum [RUL-643]), House Rules [RUL-644] and Safety Tools [RUL-645]. ## Practical gaps in the Undefined Lore Register (November 2026)
Six practical gaps from the Undefined Lore Register [DEC-901] are filled; the register's mysteries stay open. Ashvale's keeping song has its four lines, The Keeping Song [RUL-650]: players get the words, the GM the meaning, and singing it at the stones gives Advantage on a Thinning save once a day for anyone who has slept in Ashvale. The Black Tides are about 1,100 km wide, with three islands: Tollrock [LOC-903], the Kneelers [LOC-904] and Kittiwake [LOC-905]; what lives in the trench stays undefined. Distances in Aetheris [RUL-651] turn travel times into distances at about 24 miles a day; the travel times stay canon. Crossing the Black Tides [RUL-652] sets passage at the book rate (70 gp), the Shadow Pact's dark toll of one coin in ten at Tollrock, Kaelholm's catalogue of salvage, foreign goods at double the first time they cross, and a barter guide for western Kaelmar; all other prices stay at the book. Journeys in the Ethereal Realm [RUL-653] are measured in dice of days, shortened by a shared purpose, and the Black Horizon can't be reached by travel; what lies beyond it stays undefined. Kaelmar's north, south and ash barrens have named places: Hearthdeep [SET-329] and Whitecairn [SET-330]; Tumblewater [SET-331] and Mossback [SET-332]; Ashgather [SET-333] and Cinderwell [SET-334], with Kaunna [NPC-467], Harbour-mother Hettie Goodbarrel [NPC-468], Elder Gorrum [NPC-469] and Speaker Ghora Coalsong [NPC-470]. ## What this world asks of players (November 2026)
The Session Zero page [RUL-640] opens with what this world asks of players, agreed before characters are made: losing memories and names is part of play; not every fight should be fought and not every question gets answered; the party sticks together and knows each other's names; and the tone is dark and sad, with the table's lines and veils respected.

## The last open questions (November 2026)
The last open questions are answered. The three dead gods have portfolios, cleric domains, worshippers, symbols and rites [RUL-663]: Maruun Tempest or Grave, Aurex (ambition) War or Order, the Nameless Smith Forge or Knowledge; every god's handbook page now gives its cleric domains, the six living gods' from the archive. Years before the Shrouding are counted Before the Bloom (BB); Kaelith was ruled by the Chancellery, though when it began stays unknown; the Warding was in 17 BB, Kaelholm took its name in 1 AB, the Continuity was founded in 3 AB, the Spore Circle in 12 AB and its Eastern Tendril in 41 AB; the first regular crossing of the Black Tides, by the Long Dark, sailed from Mothhaven on Spring Dawn 1, 298 AB, and Captain Corr's ship is the Lodestar [RUL-664]. The Vault of the Shard of Dominion has its five rooms [RUL-665]. The legendary sites have GM-only true locations but no map pins, so the handbook still says nobody agrees where they are [RUL-666]; among them, the Nameless Forge lies beneath the Cairn of the Forgotten, and the Shattered Throne moves. Thornhaven has about 150 people, and Hollow Refuge is governed by the Lantern Council with a Warden of the Lamp, currently Mara Venn [RUL-667]. An anchor who takes someone's Thinning save makes it at the same DC and, on a failure, takes the Thinning themselves; once a day, and only for someone whose true name they know [RUL-668].

## New pins, the crossing's encounters, and the Loose Thread's finale (November 2026)
The world map now pins Tollrock, the Kneelers and Kittiwake on the Black Tides, and Hearthdeep, Whitecairn, Tumblewater, Mossback, Ashgather and Cinderwell in Kaelmar. Two encounter tables join the others: Crossing the Black Tides, and Kaelmar's far places, residents on the road. The Loose Thread has its finale, The Waystone at Dusk (levels 4 to 5): when the three ends reach the Crossroads of Fate, the Hollow Court sits, and Justicar Quell tries whether a cut thread may be retied; the party argues Lemmy's case with what they have gathered, and then chooses to tie the thread or let it go, as the arc already sets out. The Shard of Balance inside the waystone is why the world shifts when the thread is tied (GM only). At that moment every bell in the Hollow Marches rings a half-beat late, as an omen of the Return's current stage, without moving it on.

## The Eclipse: four mid-campaign adventures (November 2026)
Four adventures for levels 5 to 10 form a new arc, The Eclipse. In each, one of the Black Sun's four prophets tries for an eclipse-rite, the only way the Return moves (What the Black Sun Knows [COS-033]), and Azhir may still quietly sabotage it. Paid Twice (levels 5 to 7): Selka holds Bloodbound contracts on both sides of the Bloodmarked Fields, and Seraine gathers shard splinters there. The Sunken Nave (levels 6 to 8): the Drowned Saints dive to the Abyssal Hollow with half of Maruun's heart, and Vhalek's faithful hold their rite over the thin trench; whether the Hollow is the Saints' sunken city stays open. The First Fire (levels 7 to 9): the Ashen Covenant's fourth expedition splits between Hadrek and Thaelon, Aedrin follows whoever stands with the Fire, and Cassiel tries a rite there; the realm is thin around the First Fire, and what the Fire is stays open. Echoes in the Sand (levels 8 to 10): a summons to the Colosseum of Echoes, sent by the Black Sun through the Titans, where each character fights their echo and Isolde is ordered to perform her rite. Bouts there are to yielding; outside them, the deep realm's death and Unmaking hold.

## The Thirteen Vaults: a Kaelmar arc (November 2026)
Four adventures for levels 5 to 10 form Kaelmar's arc, The Thirteen Vaults. Each opens one vault by its own key, which no roll can replace. The Sea Between (levels 5 to 6): Saltgate's key is Captain Wilhelmina Corr of Aetheris and someone of Kaelmar; opening it makes far-speaking stones work across the Black Tides and gives up the old sea-roads. The Dreaming Fen (levels 6 to 7): Mirewell holds impressions of Kaelith minds, which have leaked out as the dreams Agathe Mudge sells; someone must give up a dream. Nobody Decides Alone (levels 7 to 9): Rimeholt's frost-writing counts down to Evernight, when the failsafes would weaken and the change slowly resume; Kaelholm, the Eastern Tendril and the Collective must all say yes to the same thing; what the Original Directive says stays the GM's to write. The Hollow Bell (levels 9 to 10): opening Hollowbell with thirteen voices gives the notes that open any one vault without its key; all thirteen opened together would wake the web and the Root Core. The Seventh Ward stirs as each vault opens; opening vaults one at a time never wakes the Root Core.

## Campaign mechanics and standard D&D (November 2026)
The app can run a standard D&D campaign as well as this setting. Each of the setting's mechanics has its own switch in Settings, with two presets, Standard D&D and The Worldkeeper setting: the setting's pages (Aetheris, Kaelmar and the Ethereal Realm in the menu, and the quick reference), Thinning with true names and memories, the Return, fading salvage, and the setting's treasure. A library holding this setting starts with all of them on. The sheet and builder list the world's peoples, classes and backgrounds first, then everything else in the GM's rules packs. Treasure and encounters can be generated from the GM's books, and the setting's data is mapped to them: each region has a terrain for encounters (the Hollow Marches grassland, hill and forest; the Southern Fens swamp and coastal; the Ash Barrens desert and grassland; the Ethereal Realm's depths ethereal and shadowfell, then astral below the Ossuary of Kings), its treasure tables' items show their rules from the books, and its fight cards carry the book stat blocks they name. Four items in the Ethereal Realm's treasure table were credited to Spelljammer but come from other books; their credits are corrected, and Star Map has rules in none. Characters are built by the 2024 rules only.

## The SRD, the setting pack and open content (November 2026)
The built-in SRD 5.2.1 is complete: besides the character rules it holds the SRD's 330 creatures, its magic items, gear, poisons, traps and magical contagions, read from the SRD itself, so a campaign runs with no books imported. The setting's own creatures, hazards and items form a second built-in pack, the Worldkeeper setting pack, off until the GM turns it on, and licensed CC BY 4.0, credited "The Worldkeeper setting pack, by the Worldkeeper contributors". It includes the rules (only rarity and effect; not lore or secrets) of the twenty-two Aetheris items whose rules were in the archive, published by the owner's decision. When the Thinning mechanic is off, nine entries use standard-rules versions: the Maintenance Echo no longer ends Thinning; the Thing from the Between's touch becomes Fraying Touch (a DC 13 Charisma save, or a level of Exhaustion only Greater Restoration removes and a memory lost for good); the Glass and the Worldscar Key cost a DC 15 Charisma save (failure: a level of Exhaustion and a memory lost for good), and the Glass's true-name protection becomes a hand held and a full name spoken; the Memory-crystal takes a lost memory or a level of Exhaustion from magic; the Name-thread turns a failed Charisma save into a success; the Glass Key opens a glass door without its save; the True-Name Mirror gives Advantage against effects that take memories; and the Shardscar Crystal leaves a glowing crystal scar (Resistance to Force damage, Disadvantage on Stealth, removed only by Wish or a level 5 Remove Curse). GMs can also bring in openly licensed content from Open5e, each source as its own pack with its licence.

## The app's design overhaul (November 2026)
The app's look and layout are being overhauled by the design brief in docs/DESIGN_OVERHAUL.md. Navigation becomes a top bar of five workspaces (Codex, Campaign, Characters, Atlas, Live) with a sidebar for the current workspace's pages, a Library menu for maintenance and Settings, and a right-hand inspector for quick looks. Warm Parchment becomes the default theme everywhere, including the published handbook and the table server, and Dark Codex stays as the alternative. During a live session the other workspaces stay visible. The app opens on the active campaign's overview when there is one. Nothing about the data, the rules or what players can see changes.

## The world map is to scale, and the travel times follow it (November 2026)
Before release, canon may change where the owner decides. The world map is drawn to scale, about 2,700 miles across (0.8 miles to a pixel of the map), and shows the known world: the two continents and the Black Tides; the world itself is larger, and the far places lie beyond its frame. The map's places stay where they are drawn and pinned, and every travel time follows them, at a normal pace on foot (about 24 miles a day) or about 50 miles a day by ship. So the Hollow Marches are about a month across: the Crossroads of Fate is about three days from Hollow Refuge, the Bloodmarked Fields about a week, and Blackveil Fortress about a week and a half. The dominions' borders are one to two weeks from the Refuge, and their capitals two to three and a half weeks (the Rootspire two, Varkhaal two and a half, Noctis nearly three, Empyrean Spire and Zepharyos three and a half). Ashvale's villages are two to four weeks from the town, and Kaelholm to Ashvale about 800 miles. The crossing of the Black Tides keeps its two weeks, about 600 miles. The archive's own travel times are corrected to match (errata), and the Loose Thread's dead man walks three days and nights to the waystone. The Ethereal Realm's map stays unscaled.

## Your own world (November 2026)
The Worldkeeper should be just as good for a GM's own world as for this setting. Realms become the GM's own: any number of worlds, continents and planes in one library, each with its own name, colour and icon. This setting's world, Aetheris, Kaelmar and the Ethereal Realm become four of them, with nothing about them changing. Every kind of record gets a template, and a new record can be made step by step or written straight in a Markdown editor. A fresh install offers a short wizard to make your own world, with a few teaching records that can be removed in one click. In a world the GM makes themselves, new records are canon at once; for an imported archive like this one, Canon Review stays as it is. Both ways of starting stand on one foundation: this setting is a setting pack (its realms, record types, templates, content and the roles its records play) with its systems as optional modules, and nothing about it is special in the app's own code, so a world of the GM's own can do anything this one can. On first run the GM chooses between building their own world and playing this setting, in the same wizard. The app keeps the name The Worldkeeper; this setting is called the Worldkeeper setting, so the first run offers "Build my own world" or "Play in the Worldkeeper setting". The plan is in docs/OWN_WORLD_PLAN.md.

## Realms, worlds and Evershroud (November 2026)
A realm and a world are different things. A realm is a plane of existence and can hold several worlds; a world holds continents, and a continent can hold region groups. The World sits in the realm of Evershroud, and Aetheris and Kaelmar are continents of The World. The Ethereal Realm is a realm beside Evershroud, not a world. The areas of the Ethereal Realm are its domains, not astral planes: the Hollow Verge is a domain of the Ethereal Realm, and the six deeper domains (the Fields of Lost Realities, the Ossuary of Kings, the Silent Archive, the Loom of Eternity, the Citadel of the Hidden and the Black Horizon) sit beneath the Hollow Verge. A record of the Ethereal Realm belongs to the domain its own text places it in (where someone lives, keeps watch or sleeps, a landmark's domain, the depth a creature is met at), and stays part of the Ethereal Realm too; the realm's cosmology, rules, factions, peoples and the people whose place isn't given stay with the realm as a whole. The app keeps to this nesting: a realm is always outermost, a world sits in a realm (or stands on its own), a continent in a world, a domain in a realm or another domain, and a region group in a continent.

## Campaigns and libraries (November 2026)
Several groups can play in one world without one group's handbook showing what another learned, and unrelated worlds can live in libraries of their own. A reveal goes either to everyone, as common knowledge in the world, or to one campaign; once a library has two or more campaigns, a new reveal goes to the active campaign unless the GM chooses everyone. Reveals made before this stay world-wide, so no handbook changes on upgrade. Maps, pins and region outlines are revealed the same way. Each campaign has its own handbook address and code on the table server, and the table server's main address keeps showing the active campaign. The published GitHub Pages handbook is a demo of the app, not where groups play: publishing stays as it is. An installation holds several libraries, one open at a time; an existing installation moves into its first library on upgrade, after a backup. The rules from the GM's books are shared by every library on the computer and imported once, with each library choosing which it uses. A book is switched on in the library it's imported in and stays off in the other libraries until the GM switches it on there; a library from before keeps every book it used. Removing a library archives it, keeping its files. The plan is in docs/LIBRARIES_PLAN.md.

## Worldbooks (November 2026)
A shareable world is a **worldbook** (what the app called a setting pack: a world's records, maps, calendar, rules, templates and adventures). Bringing one in is **adding** it, never "importing". The Worldkeeper world ships inside the app as a worldbook, pre-installed and complete, so no one has to add an archive by hand; it ships whole, GM-only secrets included, knowing the repository and the app are public and anyone (players too) can read them. GMs who build their own world can share it as a worldbook, and others add it; adding one starts a new library by default. A worldbook never contains book text, only the names of rules. Adding a worldbook says which of its books the GM is missing. Sharing one asks how its books count: required (it can't be added without them), optional (it can, and shows those things by name only), or safe mode (open rules only: anything from a closed book is swapped for the closest open-rules match, in a list the GM reviews, and open-licensed rules travel inside it). A safe-mode swap keeps the original, so a GM who has the book gets the original. A GM with the books can turn the open rules off in one switch (Books only) and use only the books. When a GM adds a worldbook and has every book it draws on, the library uses only the books, without asking (open rules stay only for a gap the books leave, said once). The archive's working notes stay out of the Worldkeeper worldbook. The plan is in docs/WORLDBOOKS_PLAN.md.

## After Worldbooks (November 2026)
The next releases take up all four open Worldbooks items: removing a worldbook from the computer, sharing adventures, random tables and loose pins, stand-ins for creatures, and the Worldkeeper world's own open species. The world gets open (CC BY 4.0) species rules for the 40 peoples that today play as a book species, so it is whole on the SRD alone. They are named after each people's own name in the world (Cat-folk, not Tabaxi), never a book species' name, since many of those names aren't open. The book wins: a GM who has the book gets the book's species, and the world's open rules are the fallback, set aside by Books only like any open rules. All 40 are written as drafts and reviewed together before they ship. Order: removing from the computer (v0.8.1), sharing adventures, tables and pins (v0.8.2), creature stand-ins (v0.8.3), the species (v0.9.0). The plan is in docs/PLAN_0.9.md.
