# The Guests

## What they are [SECRET]
Past the Black Horizon there is no ground, no time that runs one way, and nothing kind. The three teachers went out there with nothing, and to survive, and to find a way to keep the seed safe from the Watchers, they made bargains with what they found. Each brought back a Guest: an ally, a price, or both. The teachers think they understand the terms. They don't.

The Guests are not monsters to fight. They are older than the elder gods, vast, patient and incomprehensible, and they think of the world as a place they have been promised. Like the elder gods, each has Laws of Existence instead of hit points, a manifestation that can be beaten without killing it, and ways it can be turned back (see Elder Gods at the Table [RUL-621]).

## The Hunger in the Weave
*The Unknotter's Guest.*

From a distance it looks like nothing: a place where the threads of the Loom go slack. Up close it is an absence that moves with intent, the shape of every death that didn't happen, gathered into something that is hungry for the knots it was promised. Fate-seers can't look at it. Thread-grues worship it.

- **What it was promised:** every knot of death the Unknotter undoes. It means to eat the ending out of the world, so that nothing ever finishes, ever again.
- **As it nears:** wounds stop closing and stop killing. The dying linger, awake. Stories can't be finished; books end mid-sentence.
- **Its heralds:** Memory-Drinkers [CRE-541].
- **Laws of Existence** (with Immutable Existence and the Unseen Gaze [RUL-621]):
  - *Nothing Ends.* Nothing in its reach dies or heals naturally: wounds stay open, the dying stay dying. Magical healing restores only half.
  - *Unfinished.* A long rest in its reach gives only a short rest's benefits.
  - *The Starving Weave.* At the start of each round, one random caster in its reach loses its lowest unspent spell slot.
- **Manifestation:** a walking absence the size of a cathedral. Start from the Nightwalker (Monsters of the Multiverse).
- **How it can be turned back:** give it an ending it can't eat. A deathless creature (one of its own kept) who freely chooses to die, in its reach, closes its mouth: it goes back past the Horizon. Or sew shut its way in with the Loom Needle [ITM-022].

## The Choir Between Angles
*The Door-Maker's Guest.*

It lives in the corners of things, in the angle between two walls that is a little more than it should be. Seen whole (and nobody has), it would be a cathedral of right angles that all point at you, singing in a key that makes geometry agree with it. It is the reason the Door-Maker's door back in is taking so long: it keeps adding rooms.

- **What it was promised:** a house. The world, rebuilt to its proportions, with every room full.
- **As it nears:** rooms are bigger inside than out, then smaller. Corridors in the Citadel, then in old houses in the world, lead somewhere new. People hear a choir in the walls at night.
- **Its heralds:** Angle-Walkers [CRE-542].
- **Laws of Existence** (with Immutable Existence and the Unseen Gaze [RUL-621]):
  - *Geometry Agrees.* All ground in its reach is difficult terrain, and anything that flies or teleports arrives 1d4 × 10 feet off course.
  - *The Choir.* Concentration saves have Disadvantage while the Choir can be heard. Deafened creatures are immune, and can't hear anything else either.
  - *Rooms Added.* Each time the party rests in its reach, the place has one more room and one fewer way out.
- **Manifestation:** a cathedral of angles that sings. Start from the Elder Brain (Monster Manual), raised to about CR 20, with its lair a building that rearranges each round.
- **How it can be turned back:** it can't abide a true circle. Sing the keeping song inside an unbroken ring of standing stones (Ashvale's Rite Circle [LOC-301] has thirteen) and the Choir can't add a room to it, or follow anyone out. Hold the circle until dawn, and it goes.

## The Shepherd of Quiet Stars
*The One Who Called Out's Guest.*

It answered her call, as something once did before. It is a pale shape among the far stars, too big to see except as the stars it hides, and it moves the way a shepherd moves: slowly, patiently, gathering. Wherever it has been, the stars are quiet. It says it can stand against the Watchers, and it may be telling the truth, the way a wolf can stand against other wolves.

- **What it was promised:** the flock. The seed, gathered, kept and safe, forever, in its fold, whether the seed wants it or not.
- **As it nears:** stars go out over the Black Horizon, then over the world. People sleep more, and dream of a warm field. Some don't wake, and look peaceful.
- **Its heralds:** Voices from Beyond [CRE-543].
- **Laws of Existence** (with Immutable Existence and the Unseen Gaze [RUL-621]):
  - *Gentle Sleep.* Each hour in its reach, a creature makes a Wisdom save or falls asleep. Only hearing its true name spoken by someone who knows it wakes it.
  - *Quiet Stars.* No light falls from the sky in its reach, and darkvision reaches half as far.
  - *The Fold.* A creature asleep in its reach for an hour is carried off by a Voice from Beyond, into the fold.
- **Manifestation:** a pale shape among the stars, many-limbed and patient. Start from the Kraken (Monster Manual), swimming the dark between stars instead of the sea.
- **How it can be turned back:** refuse the fold. A whole town keeps a vigil, every one of them awake and calling each other by name through one night, and the Shepherd learns the flock won't come. Or the Veilborn's book of the remembered, read aloud whole, wakes everyone it has taken.

## What they mean for the world
If the Guests come through, the people are not destroyed. They are kept, forever unfinished, rebuilt or herded, by things that think they are being kind. That is the horror of it. Whether the Watchers are worse is a question no one in the world can answer.
